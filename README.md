# Tony Khan Simulator

A wrestling general-manager game: draft from AEW and WWE's rosters, book Dynamite and Collision through a full year — a PPV every 4 weeks, alternating AEW and WWE's biggest shows from the Royal Rumble through All In to Survivor Series — and win the fan war. Works fully offline once installed.

## Put it on GitHub Pages
1. Create a new public repository on GitHub (e.g. `all-elite-gm`).
2. Upload every file in this folder to the repository root (**Add file → Upload files**).
3. Go to **Settings → Pages**, set **Source** to *Deploy from a branch*, choose `main` and `/ (root)`, and save.
4. After a minute your game is live at `https://<your-username>.github.io/all-elite-gm/`.

## Install on your phone
- **iPhone (Safari):** open the link, tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**.
- **Android (Chrome):** open the link, tap **⋮ → Install app** (or *Add to Home screen*).

Open it once while online; after that it launches with no connection.

## Updating the game
Upload the changed files (usually `index.html`, plus any `.js` files that changed, such as `season.js` or `tutorial.js`), then open `sw.js` and bump the version in `VERSION` (e.g. `aegm-v36` → `aegm-v37`) so phones pick up the update. Installed copies update the next time they're opened online (close and reopen the app once).

## Play online with a friend
Host a league for 2–4 GMs and friends join with a 6-letter code. Everyone runs their own brand, you face a different GM each week, and the most fans gained by Survivor Series wins. Everyone creates wrestlers and books each week at the same time (the week airs when the last GM locks in); the draft goes in order, and you can queue up to 10 picks so they're made for you the moment you're on the clock. Offline changes sync later, and each player gets a notification when it's their move. One-time setup: see [ONLINE-SETUP.md](ONLINE-SETUP.md).

## New players: the tour
A new game offers a one-minute **draft tour**, then a short **first-week tour** once the draft is done: your show and how you win, Needs your attention, Suggest the rest, booking by feel, feuds and going live. Everything else is a one-time **coach tip** that pops up the first time it matters — the match editor, your first results, PPV week, the AEW/WWE calendar, the running order, show flow, stamina, the Roster, Free agents, Titles and Office screens — at most one booking tip a week so they never pile up. Replay it any time from **⚙️ → Rookie orientation** (where you can also turn coach tips on or off).

The tour reads its numbers straight from the game and is checked automatically on every update (`tools/check-tutorial.py`), so it keeps up as the rules change. When a stop changes, players who already took the tour get a short **What's new** replay of just those stops.

## AEW meets WWE
- **One talent pool.** WWE's Raw and SmackDown rosters (current to Money in the Bank, Oct 10, 2026) are in the draft and free agency alongside AEW's. Ratings reflect each wrestler's current ability and popularity on the same scale — WWE's biggest names are the most popular wrestlers in the game. Real WWE tag teams and factions come together (The Bloodline, The Vision, Judgment Day, LWO, The Lucha Bros…), and anyone currently hurt or on leave starts out injured in an existing save.
- **Supplemental draft for older saves.** A save that started before the WWE update can hold a one-time WWE Supplemental Draft from the Home screen: 3 rounds, only the unsigned WWE wrestlers, the brand behind in the fan war picks first, and a budget on top of each brand's payroll with room for two big stars and a solid third pick. It runs on the normal draft screen and drops back into the same week; anyone not picked stays a free agent. You can skip it instead.
- **Photos.** 🔍 Search for a WWE wrestler looks on wwe.com for their official photo; in **Quick photo mode**, tap **WWE** to run through just the WWE roster.
- **A bigger draft budget.** The draft salary cap is 20% higher than before (about $527K/wk in a two-GM game), so each brand can afford more top stars for star-vs-star matches. Revenue grows only half as much, so the extra payroll is a real cost and the bank stays tighter.
- **WWE's world titles** — Undisputed WWE, World Heavyweight, WWE Women's and WWE Women's World — join the ten AEW belts, so each show starts with seven.
- **Two supershows.** WrestleMania (week 12) and All In (week 24) are the climaxes of the WWE and AEW halves of the year: hotter crowds, double the fan swing, bigger gates and bigger venue bills.
- **The calendar** alternates the biggest shows: Royal Rumble, Revolution, WrestleMania, Double or Nothing, Money in the Bank, All In, SummerSlam, All Out, Full Gear and Survivor Series, which closes the year. The Owen Hart Cups now end at Double or Nothing and the Continental Classic at Full Gear.
- **🏟️ Royal Rumble.** A men's (8) and a women's (6) Rumble lock onto your Royal Rumble card, which runs five matches in all (Money in the Bank runs six). You pick the entrants (Clear entrants empties the field), the winner and who's eliminated last — the runner-up gets a small popularity bump instead of a loss. The winner earns a world title shot at WrestleMania against your best champion in their division (belts never leave their show, so if yours are vacant the winner is first in line when one is crowned).
- **💼 Money in the Bank.** A men's (6) and a women's (5) ladder match lock onto your Money in the Bank card. The winner holds the briefcase for a year and can cash in on a world champion of their division on your brand at any time: book them against the champion and make it the cash-in, or open any match the champion is in and have the holder cash in straight after it. You decide whether the cash-in works; it lifts the match it follows (+½★ when it succeeds). Unused briefcases expire at the next Money in the Bank, and the rival cashes in too.
- **Survivor Series is the crossover show** (it took Forbidden Door's place): up to two dream matches against the rival's stars, and rival invasions in the TV weeks before it.
- **Existing saves** keep their week: the WWE wrestlers arrive in free agency, WWE's titles start vacant (two for each brand), and the PPVs take their new names from here on.

## The calendar
- **📅 Always know where you are.** The strip on the Home screen shows every week of the year (PPVs in gold, supershows brightest, this week outlined). Tap it, or **WK** in the header, for the full calendar: each PPV and what's special about it, TV specials, tournament rounds, crossover season, promised matches and the week each of your contracts runs out.

## The countdown

Now and then a countdown clock appears during a solo promo. Three weeks later a free agent from the other side of the face/heel line debuts and goes after the wrestler who had the mic (instant heat), and you get one week to sign them at a discount — bigger if the promo hyped the clock. Sign them early, or let the rival, and they debut for whoever did.

## Scouting & ROH (the farm system)
- **Three scouting boards** (Roster → Free agents → Scouting), refreshing every few weeks: **🌱 Indies** (raw prospects and gimmick acts, the most upside), **🏟️ ROH** (polished regulars plus the real ROH roster — Jay Lethal, Matt Sydal, Top Flight, the Von Erichs, Premier Athletes, Dalton Castle, Lacey Lane and more. They aren't in the AEW draft, so ROH is the only place to get them. Ready sooner, less upside) and **🌏 Overseas** (skilled imports from Japan, Mexico and the UK who are still learning promos).
- **Known names from the PWI 500.** Every Indies and Overseas board also carries one or two real names from outside AEW and WWE — men ranked on the 2026 PWI 500 (Leon Slater, Nic Nemeth, Mustafa Ali, Michael Oku, Yota Tsuji, Zack Sabre Jr., Templario, Atlantis Jr. and more, with their rank on the card) plus top independent and joshi women (Masha Slamovich, Sareee, Saya Kamitani, Starlight Kid, Lady Flammer…). They cost more than prospects but arrive closer to ready.
- **ROH books itself every week.** Everyone on your ROH roster works a match on HonorClub against ROH regulars or other GMs' prospects, builds an ROH record and grows toward their potential. ROH's four titles (World, Women's World, TV, Pure) are defended every other week by the best records. Wins, good matches and ROH gold build **call-up buzz** (🧊 → 🔥), which fades if they cool off. When someone is hot, the briefing says it's time to bring them up — and the hotter they are, the bigger their AEW debut. The ROH screen shows the champions, last week's results, the standings and your watchlist.
- **Tapes & emails.** Most weeks a prospect sends you their tape or an email — it lands in the line outside your office at the start of the week. Pass, **keep following** them (up to 6 — they send updates as they improve, get a little pricier, and may sign elsewhere), or sign them straight to ROH.
- **Sign to AEW or to ROH.** AEW signings take a roster spot and can be booked right away. ROH signings go to your ROH roster (Roster → 🏟️ ROH, up to 8): no AEW roster spot, half salary, and they grow toward their potential every week on ROH TV, slower than the Performance Center.
- **Feature** one ROH wrestler to grow faster and build popularity (up to 45). Send someone on a once-per-career **10-week excursion**: NJPW (biggest in-ring jump and a tougher body), CMLL (comes back a bigger draw) or RevPro (real mic reps). They return with more potential and a bigger debut pop.
- **Call up** anyone whenever you need them (they get a debut pop), and **send down** rookies or anyone at 45 popularity or lower to develop. Champions can't be sent down. ROH deals run out like any contract — extend them from the ROH screen.
- **Teams reunite.** Sign or call up both halves of a real ROH team (Top Flight, the Von Erichs, Premier Athletes…) and they're a team again on your roster.
- Works in online leagues too: every GM has their own ROH roster.

## Offers & contracts
- **Free agents with 55+ popularity want an offer**, not just a fee: a signing bonus, a weekly salary, and promises — 🎬 creative control, ⭐ main event spots, 🏆 a title shot, 📺 a place on every PPV. Each wrestler wants different things (a big star wants control, a hungry midcarder wants main events), and the offer screen tells you how it looks to them. Smaller names still sign on the spot.
- **Sealed bids.** Solo, the rival may send an offer too and the wrestler picks right away. In a league, every other GM can send one sealed counter before the week airs, and the wrestler picks the best package when it does. On party night the TV reveals every offer in a "Signing war" before the results.
- **Contract countdown.** Deals with 5 weeks or less left (9 for stars) show on a ✍️ card on Home and at the top of the weekly briefing, amber at 3 weeks and red on the last show. The Book screen repeats it, and going live on someone's last show asks you to re-sign them or let them go first
- **Re-signing is a negotiation** (from the weekly briefing or the wrestler's card). They make demands; agree to some or all, or counter on money. They take it, counter back (up to three rounds) or walk away, depending on their morale, how hot your show is and whether you've kept your word before.
- **Promises are contract terms.** The Book screen shows what's owed. Keep one and morale rises (and a little lost trust comes back); break one and morale drops; after three, a wrestler may walk out. An injury pauses the clock on a promise. Only true main eventers ask for creative control, it lasts 12 weeks, and a roster can have at most 3 people with it. **Creative control** means they won't agree to lose. Booked to lose, they show on the checklist with **Talk to them** (and again at go-live): offer to protect them in the finish (a cheat, an upset roll-up or a count-out instead of a clean pin), a big win down the line (a main-event or PPV win within 4 weeks), or their win back (beat the same opponent by the next PPV). Each is easier to sell than the last; morale, broken promises, how hot your show is and who they're losing to all count. Go live without a deal and about half the time they change the finish and go over (−¼★); otherwise they do the job under protest and morale drops hard. A promised win stays on the checklist until it happens — miss the deadline and it's a broken promise.

## Booking by feel
- **Match vs wrestler, at a glance.** Anything about the match (Talent, Chem, Story, Extras — in the buzz bar, Match details and on booked slots) is a gold segmented meter; a wrestler's own ratings (Ring, Star) are silver dots in a box hanging off their card, topped in their face/heel color, with how they match up (Chem, Feud) under a "VS …" divider.
- **The crowd buzz bar.** Once both sides are picked, the match editor shows the crowd buzz and the four ingredient pips (Talent, Chem, Story, Extras) in a bar that pins to the top as you scroll — change the stipulation, finish or run-in and watch it move. The full Match details, with the reason behind each row, stay at the bottom.
- **✨ Suggest the rest is the floor, not the ceiling.** It covers the must-dos (promised and contracted matches, contract clauses, promises from pitches, the most overdue title defense, the weekly goal, the crowd's chant) and fills the rest with a plain card — standard matches, clean finishes, no run-ins, no PPV announcements, empty bonus slots. In the balance sweep a Suggest-only GM on Normal wins about 30% of nights and about half its seasons; a hands-on booker (`--engaged`) wins clearly more.
- **Match details.** Every match shows its ingredients — Talent, Chemistry, Story and Extras, up to five pips each with the reason beside it — plus anything holding it back. Promos show Talent, Fit, Story and your live calls. Under each wrestler in the editor: ring (or mic) and star pips, morale, stamina and anything they've asked for. Tap **?** by Chemistry for the style chart. The wrestler picker can sort by 🏆 Champions and 🎤 Requests.
- **Needs your attention.** The Book screen's checklist of everything that matters for this show, checked live against the card. Anything still blocking a clean go-live sits on top, the rest under Everything else: Before you go live (slots still to book, requests, promised matches, promises, contracts ending tonight — going live with one open asks you first), Chances tonight, Keep in mind (including one row for every title that needs defending, led by the most overdue) and Front office (including a warning when your roster runs thin). Most items have a button straight to the fix.
- **The briefing is a rundown.** A count up top, then *Before you go live*, *Chances tonight*, *Backstage* and the *Front office*. Each place backstage is one card with one voice: unhappy wrestlers are chips in *The hallway* (with their mood), tired ones in *The trainer's room*, overdue titles are rows with their own Defend button, and every contract coming up is one *On the phone* list, soonest first, with what's at stake (a title, the briefcase, an unpaid feud). Advice shared by a whole card is said once, and anything new since last week is tagged NEW.
- **Every screen reads at a glance (v201).** Book shows each segment's buzz and tags (tap **Show details** for the pips), folds the bill and selling out into one line and puts **Go live** at the bottom of the card. Home has one briefing strip, the contracts that need a decision, the top three feuds and the latest five Wire stories (**Show all** for the rest). Results lead each segment with its headline note, fold the rival's show and the box office, and only list news that isn't already shown. Titles are compact rows (rival's folded), the wrestler card puts training and turns up top with one set of stat bars and the top three matchups, a feud opens on its two decisions and heat curve, the calendar folds the weeks already played, the match editor shows chemistry and heat per pairing with Save pinned, and the Office, Settings and Free agents keep their explanations one tap away.
- **Repeats are like for like.** A singles rematch only counts as a repeat of last week's singles match, and a multi-person match only of last week's multi-person one, so a Rumble doesn't spoil next week's one-on-one.
- **No star math while booking.** The match editor shows crowd buzz (🥶 → 🔥🔥🔥) and ▲/▼ tells on titles and finishes instead of exact star bonuses. Stipulations show a rough upside (📈 big / some / little, or 📉 works against them) weighed against the extra wear (🔋), injury risk (🩹) and anyone it would leave gassed. Risky calls (first-time pairings, gimmick matches, screwy finishes, run-ins, rookies) make a match a 🎲 wild card.
- **Rumbles and gauntlets don't settle feuds.** Rivals who meet in a Royal Rumble, Money in the Bank or the Casino Gauntlet build heat, but the feud still needs its own match to be blown off — even on a PPV.
- **💥 TV blow-offs.** A feud at 35+ heat doesn't have to wait for a PPV: in the match editor, tap **Make it the blow-off**. A clean win (or an upset) ends it on weekly TV, and a gimmick stipulation makes it a fitting end with a bigger morale and popularity boost. A dirty finish or no contest leaves it unsettled. It's always a smaller payoff than a PPV blow-off.
- **Pick who takes the pin.** In any match with three or more wrestlers, only one person on the losing end takes the fall and everyone else stays protected. The match editor lets you choose who (the default is the lowest-billed), and shows how much of a rub the winner gets for pinning them. A wrestler with creative control only objects if they're the one taking the pin.
- **Titles are split evenly.** A new game deals the titles out between the two shows (each gets seven, with at least one men's and one women's world title on each side — AEW's and WWE's world titles are spread across both). Each brand crowns its own vacant titles, and every belt stays on its show for good — there are no cross-show title challenges, so a belt can't be stolen. A champion who leaves the show (traded, poached by tampering, released) vacates the title, and Rumble, Money in the Bank, Casino Gauntlet and Owen Hart Cup shots are always against your own champions.
- **The pay-per-view look (v222).** Every screen, pop-up and the party-night TV share one visual system: a single slant on every shape (panels, buttons, chips, tags, photo cards, avatars — no chamfered corners, rounded pills or dashed boxes), flat fills instead of glows and gradients, and heavy italic type. Gold is what you act on and what's yours, red is live and the main event. The show is a full-width banner with a slanted bottom edge — red for Dynamite, blue for Collision, gold for a pay-per-view — and every screen's header is the same banner in charcoal with a gold slash. Book slots are a gold number tab (red for the main event) beside a slanted plate, with **Suggest the rest** and **Clear card** right under the running order. A colored top edge marks a panel's kind (gold = yours, red = needs you); face/heel colors and champions' gold frames follow the slanted edge. The tab bar has a gold pill that glides between tabs, banners wipe in along the slant, and big moments (debuts, new champions) slam the name onto a red plate under a gold tab. Live promos and matches (v225) open with a compact red banner and **face-off plates**: each side once, with its photos, its name, one gold line (title, team or record) and a face/heel stripe, VS between two sides (& for a promo pairing) and a grid for three or more. The app icon (v223) is **Fan war**: a white italic TK over the brand-war rope, your red end on the left and the gold knot pulled your way (rebuild it with `python3 tools/make-icon.py`).
- **The broadcast look (v212–215).** The game's look takes its style from the star reveal while keeping the glowing show titles, the calendar strip and photo cards: quiet tracked section labels without emoji, a colored left edge on every panel (gold for yours, grey for neutral, red for things that need you and the main event), the rope for the brand war and segmented pips for PPV hype. The Book screen keeps its classic slots (gold number bar, gold label, red for the main event, the red Go live) with the crowd buzz as five pips beside each label. The slot frame is slim (a narrow number bar and a small label) so booked wrestlers get the width: they face off side by side as large plates — a big overall, the name in large type, WIN on the booked winner or a Fresh / Tired / mood chip, and the title strip. A side of two or more is a team card with the team's name — or the members' last names joined, biggest first, when they aren't a set tag team or faction — the average overall, WIN, any shared title and every member as a chip with their overall (amber when tired or hurt). Tags, match type and buzz share one line under the plates, and drag-to-swap works on every name. Tournament matches get plates too. A **Compact** toggle above the running order (remembered on the device) shrinks every slot to one-line plates — overall and last name, a small title or partners line, the winner in a gold frame and gold name, an amber dot for anyone tired — with just the key tag and buzz underneath, so a full card fits on about one screen. On photo cards only champions get a gold frame; the overall number carries the gold / silver / bronze tier (88+ / 78+ / below).
- **Reveal after the show.** Live promos end on the crowd's reaction (🥶 → 🔥🔥🔥) rather than a star rating, so every number waits for the results. In solo play the results come out as a **tug of war you advance by tapping**. Each round, your segment and the rival's in the same slot of the card face off as name plates (overall, face/heel stripe, name, partners and title — tags show each side's biggest name, Rumbles and multi-person matches show the winner and the biggest name they beat). The winner's plate lights up with a WIN tag and the loser greys out, both rows of stars fill in star by star, the better segment gets · Wins, and a rope between the two shows lurches toward it. The scoreboard keeps a running "so far" for both shows. A tap plays the next round, or finishes the one that's playing. The main event always comes last: the room dims, it holds on a drumroll, stamps its stars slowly and runs straight into the final ratings, the winner of the night and best of the night. **Skip ▸▸** jumps to the end, and **Full results** opens the whole breakdown. **Last Show** goes straight to the full results, with a **▶ Replay the reveal** button. Then tap **Why that rating?** under any of your matches to see what each part was worth, including the luck of the night.
- **Every promo has a job.** Callout (can get a match made), Sneak Attack (most heat, counts as physical contact), Contract Signing (books the PPV match), Sit-Down Interview (biggest morale lift and fastest mic growth — the fix for an unhappy wrestler), Faction Rally (the bigger star, listed first, rubs off on a less popular partner; alone, it lifts the whole group's spirits), Backstage Vignette (low risk, and grows wrestlers at popularity 60 or below faster), Champion's Address (raises the title's prestige when it lands) and Heel / Face Turn. The promo picker marks ✓ Good fit when one suits who you've picked, and Suggest the rest and the rival use the whole set.
- **Suggested tag teams (v227).** Pair the same wrestlers in three tag or trios matches without making them a team, or let an unnamed pairing win tag or trios gold, and the game suggests making it official — in the briefing's Front office section and on the Front office's Tag teams & factions card — with a generated name (plus a couple of alternatives and the members' last names) that you can tap or edit before saving. **Not a team** drops the suggestion; a later title win can still bring it back once.
- **Discovered chemistry.** Wrestlers who've never shared a ring only show chemistry "on paper" with a ❓; their first match rolls a spark that sticks as history.
- **A bonus match.** Weekly TV has an optional bonus match alongside the bonus promo. Book it when you have something worth showing or leave it empty — it counts toward the show rating like any segment and costs stamina like any match. ✨ Suggest the rest leaves it for you to decide.
- **💪 Squash matches.** Pick **Squash** in the match editor for a quick, one-sided win. It never rates highly (about 2½★ at most), so it's momentum at the cost of show quality — the bonus match is a natural home for it. Tap **Use local enhancement talent** to feed your wrestler an unsigned nobody from free agency (it costs nothing), or pick someone from your roster: the winner gets more out of beating a name, but the loser loses popularity and morale, and the more popular they are, the more it costs (a burial). The same wrestler squashing again within six weeks gets 60%, then 30%, then nothing, and squashing someone more popular than the winner backfires.
- **Stipulation specialties.** In the match editor, stipulations that suit someone's style are marked ⭐ with their name (e.g. ⭐ Specialty · Ospreay on a Ladder match), listed first, and the closed picker tells you how many there are. Ones that work against a style get a ⚠️. A booked match on a specialty stipulation shows the ⭐ on the Book screen.
- **Money stays meaningful.** A bigger company pays bigger wages when wrestlers sign or re-sign (about +15% at 1M fans), production and stipulations cost more in bigger venues, and past a big night's box office the extra comes in at half — so a hit promotion's bank levels off instead of piling up millions. The offline rival doesn't pay the wage premium (it runs a bigger roster); in online leagues every GM does. Selling out scales the same way: sponsor money grows with the company and so does the fan cost, so it's about the same trade at any size.
- **The top is hard to reach and hard to hold.** Popularity gains slow sharply past 80 and again past 90, and stars at 85+ slip a little in weeks they aren't featured (main event, PPV win, or a 3½★+ match or promo).
- **Multi-person matches** are carried by their top two names and their hottest rivalry, reward a mix of styles (triple threats and 4-ways love flyers and showmen; tags lean on partners who click), and pay off later: only one loser takes the fall, lower names gain popularity from the top star, and the top star uses less stamina.
- **TV fits the screen (v242).** Every TV screen (lobby, draft, week, live segments, both reveals, season end) now fits on one screen with no scrolling at 1080p and 4K: the live segment scales to fit, reveal rows stay compact with only the segment being revealed opened up, and small labels have a TV-sized minimum.
- **Battle Royale reveal (v241).** In 3- and 4-GM leagues the TV reveal is a battle royale: the undercard plays side by side (main events held back), then from last place up each show plays its main event and goes over the top rope. The final two settle it with a tug of war on their main events, the runner-up goes over the top rope too, and the winner stands alone on the podium with the full placings. Places are the real final results.
- **Everyone competes every week (v239–v240).** New 3- and 4-GM leagues drop the round robin: every show airs against all the others and the night is ranked. Placings are bonus-only, like a head-to-head: with 3 GMs it's +4K / +2K / 0, with 4 it's +4K / +2.5K / +1K / 0, and 1st takes the ratings win. The best main event of the night takes the final quarter-hour (+3K). Nobody loses fans for their placing; only a bad show costs you, same as always. The TV shows every GM side by side and reveals all the shows round by round; your phone's results show the night's placings. Leagues started before v239 keep their round robin.
- **Party night sweep (v238).** League and party-night results on your phone use the same tidy layout as solo (your matchup up top with the winner, each segment's headline note with the rest folded, other matchups folded under "Elsewhere"). The "Locked in" notice no longer stops your phone hearing it's your turn to go live, the locked-in toast no longer pops up on every screen refresh, an unanswered draft-tour offer no longer hangs over your live show, the TV only labels a pick on the screen that shows how it played out, and single and big-group plates scale up on the TV.
- **🎆 Production sits above Go live (v237).** Standard, Pyro & video or Full spectacle is always open right above the Go live button, with what each costs and adds to the show, and a gold edge when you can afford a boost and haven't picked one. **📌 Use for every TV show** (or every PPV) keeps your pick week after week whenever the bank can cover it, stepping down a tier when it can't.
- **The line outside your office opens itself (v237).** It forms whenever Home or Book is on screen and someone's waiting (after the results screen too), and Home's strip clears the moment the last person is answered.
- **The riskier the call, the bigger the swing (v236).** In-match calls and promo calls now win and lose by their odds: every choice shows its odds plus 🛡️ Small, ⚖️ Medium, 🔥 Big or 💥 Huge swing. A Safe bet (80%+) adds about ¼★ or costs about ⅙★; Risky (around 50%) about +⅔★ / −⅖★; a Long shot can add up to a full star or cost up to about ¾★ — rare, about 1 option in 100. Safe bet through Risky pay off about the same on average (+0.15–0.2★); long shots cost a little on average. Promo calls now land after the rating curve, so each moves the promo by exactly its swing (top talkers keep their edge directly). Situations and locker-room whispers keep their own written payoffs.
- **Promo crowd buzz is calibrated (v235).** It used to run almost a full star high (🔥🔥 promos averaged about 3★), because it skipped the curve every promo goes through. It's now fitted to simulated promos played with sensible calls — talent, the lead talker's mic, the promo type, a too-soon turn, a boiling-over attack — and lands within a quarter star on average. A single promo still swings about half a star with the calls and the crowd. Because promos rate a little below matches, most show 👍 or 🔥; 🔥🔥 is reserved for real top talkers.
- **🩸 Blood & Guts night leads the Book screen (v234).** On week 22 the Book poster carries the special's own name, a Blood & Guts card says what's special about tonight, whether the war is booked, and **Book the war** sets one up in the main event (trios, Blood & Guts stipulation). The stipulation list puts it first and tags it *Tonight only*, and the booked slot carries a red edge.
- **Match calls fit the match (v234).** In-match decisions never offer a finish the match can't have: no covers, near-falls or roll-ups in ladder, tables, Last Man Standing, "I Quit", coffin, submission or Blood & Guts matches or a Royal Rumble, and no tap-outs where a submission can't win it. Locker room whispers follow the same rule.
- **🩸 Blood & Guts, once a year (v232).** Week 22 is the Blood & Guts TV special and the only night the Blood & Guts stipulation can be booked — one trios or 8-man match per card. The briefing flags it three weeks out and again on the night, the calendar marks it, and the rival books its own when it has the roster and the money.
- **📣 Match moments and crowd reactions (v231).** Every in-match call has been rewritten to read like a real broadcast moment, drawn from big spots, controversial finishes and botches across AEW, WWE, WCW, ECW and beyond. Each call now has four crowd reactions: it lands; it lands and the wrestler is elite at it (a bigger, more specific pop — "This is awesome!", "Fight forever!", a standing ovation); it misses; or it misses and the wrestler is weak at it (a real botch — a slipped rope, a short dive, a finisher that doesn't break the table). Twenty new moments join them: screwjob finishes, slow counts, a pin with the shoulder up, a hidden elbow (or something metal under the pad), masked attackers and rivals in the crowd, a snapped top rope, a ring that collapses, stretcher spots, sleeper arm-drops, stolen finishers, chairs from the crowd, spears through the barricade, a crooked referee, "One more time!", Royal Rumble apron saves, legs buckling under the finisher and finishers that go wrong for real. The disaster-in-gorilla calls got the same treatment. Ratings and odds of the existing calls are unchanged — only the words.
- **🗣️ Promo voices (v229).** Every promo outcome is now what was actually said, in the voice of whoever is talking. Each option has four versions: a great talker (mic 80+, Mouthpiece counts) who lands it gets a specific, personal line with a pause and a hard finish; anyone else who lands it gets a clear, solid line; an average or great talker who misses goes long, generic or wrong for the moment; and a weak talker (mic under 60) who misses tangles their words, names the wrong show or has a line turn on them. The rival's comebacks follow the same voices, and great talkers mostly get the great lines. Ratings and odds are unchanged — only the words.
- **Promos on the party TV (v251).** The promo showdown now has its own TV layout: the live screen takes the full width, both wrestlers stand on the stage side by side (whoever isn't talking dims), and the lines, calls and results sit beside them in big type, so a whole promo fits a 1080p picture without scrolling (it shrinks to fit smaller TVs and scales up on 4K). It keeps one size for the whole promo instead of jumping page to page, the "picked" banner is compact, the empty box behind a shout is hidden, and a wrestler whose photo is a private upload shows their initials on the TV instead of a blank card. Checked at 4K too (v252): the picked call's words now stay clear of the ✓ PICKED badge.
- **Promo and match logic sweep (v256).** Thousands of promos and main events were played headlessly and read for lines that contradict what just happened. An opening that ends the segment now ends it: a contract signing that breaks into a pull-apart brawl, a table flipped into a pull-apart brawl, a fight called down to the ring, a walk-out, a chair shot followed by walking off — no more answers, exchanges or crowd moments after the locker room has cleared the ring (the rival's segments follow the same rule). A twist that takes the closer's place after a back-and-forth is now one that fits a confrontation with the other wrestler standing right there (security, a run-in, the other wrestler's music, the mic cutting out) — never "You deserve it!", a kid in a costume or a sing-along. Sneak attacks get their own aftermath twists, and while the target is down nothing needs them up and talking. Once the match is made — a contract on the table or a challenge already accepted — nobody asks for a yes again, and after a landed handshake or show of respect the closer stays respectful (no boil-over or knife-twist; a new "Shake on it"). Studio and backstage twists no longer mention the ring or the crowd, a faction rally isn't interrupted by the faction it's rallying, and twists where the other wrestler walks out only come when they weren't already standing there. In the main event, the big moment is about the same two people the showdown shows (no pinning one opponent while the moment kicks out the other), the booth doesn't cheer an injury stoppage, and tapping while the camera is still moving no longer skips a line, in matches or promos.
- **Main event calls only, and a match decision sweep (v255).** Undercard matches no longer stop for a random call, so a show moves faster: only the main event showdown has calls. Undercard matches still stop when something forces it — a hurt, gassed or unhappy wrestler, a grudge you told them to settle in the ring, or a whisper you approved — and the rival's undercard follows the same rule. Every match moment was swept for how it fits the showdown: each call's outcome lines were read and the ones that end the match (a three count, a tap, a bell, a ten count, a DQ, a prize pulled down…) are now known to the game. A moment plays in the heat only with calls that keep the match going; a moment whose calls end it is saved for the finish, and a finish call that keeps it going is followed by the winner putting it away. General finishes (a ref stoppage, a slow count) only end pinfall and submission matches, and a moment whose setup already ended it never gets a second finish. Moment lines now page by sentence with the camera on whoever starts the sentence (no more misread quotes), the intro names both teams (and calls the Rumble, Gauntlet and Money in the Bank by name), it only talks about bad blood when there is some, the near-fall call shows its swing, and the read-the-crowd tags are re-worked for the calls you actually get.
- **🎬 Match showdown (v254).** The main event of every live show plays like the promo showdown: both sides on the stage with the camera whipping between them, a commentary booth (play-by-play and colour) calling it, shouts across the screen (OH MY GOD!, KICKED OUT!, THREE!) and a count stamp on the cover. You call it in three acts: the **opening** tactic (fly out of the gate, chain wrestle, start a fight, throw them around, play to the crowd, a face flurry or a heel jump before the bell — the one that fits your winner's style comes first and has better odds), the **heat** (the match's big moment — the same moments, situations and disasters as before, always one in the main event; when that moment is a finish, it's saved for the end and the heat gets a tactic call instead), the **near fall** (kick out at 2.9 for more drama and a bigger finish at +3 stamina for everyone, or stay down and end it there) and the **finish** (only finishes that match allows: covers and roll-ups, rope breaks and taps, "I quit", ladders, tables, ten counts, coffins, Blood & Guts surrenders, Rumble eliminations). After the bell you pick the closing shot (celebration, handshake, a post-match beatdown or a heel jumping the winner, a staredown) — it moves heat and popularity, not stars. New calls swing by their odds like every other call, scaled down because there are more of them, and together never move the match more than ¾★ either way. The rival's main event runs the same calls by the rival's rules. It all mirrors to party-night TV. Other matches keep their single call.
- **Promo flow sweep (v253).** Every line in a back-and-forth was checked against the calls that can follow it, and every call against the answers it can get. Answers now fit what actually happened: a shove gets a shove-sized reaction (no bloody lip from a mic to the chest), a stare-down line never follows a laugh or a yawn, "nice speech" never follows a silent face-off, a dodge or "maybe next week" never follows a challenge that landed, and nobody warmly thanks you after leaving your hand hanging. Walk-away comebacks only show when the other wrestler is actually walking away, a wrestler laid out on the floor ends the segment instead of trading lines, studio-only lines (chairs, tables) stay in the studio, and a backstage callout gets its own laid-out, boil-over and hold-the-moment versions. When the other wrestler's big line lands, you answer that line instead of them getting a second one on top of it. A few new answers fill gaps (a belt in the challenger's face, a brawl security stops, a refused handshake, studio fights).
- **Promos that follow (v250).** A full sweep of how promo lines lead into each other. Nobody answers a wrestler who has walked off: when a call ends with someone leaving (your wrestler storms out, the other one slips away and you don't stop them) or flat on the mat, the exchange ends there instead of carrying on with a reply and a closer, and a twist never needs someone who's gone. Answers fit what was actually said — a handshake that already happened isn't refused, a demand to kneel gets told where to go, fine print signed unread gets a shrug, a counter after a missed attack doesn't replay the counter, tag partners don't get rival-only twists, and a line is never echoed straight back. On screen, lines go to the right speaker (accented names, "A and B" moments, "{A}'s music hits", dashes around a quote, the announcer or a fan speaking), reactions play out in full instead of being cut to their first sentence, and the other wrestler no longer gets a second last word right after landing their big line.
- **Every promo is spoken (v249).** About 2,600 outcomes used to be narration only ("A gritty training montage. They look ready for war.") — in rallies, attacks, vignettes, turns and mid-promo twists, roughly a third of promos had nobody say a word. Every one now has dialogue: your wrestler speaks on every landed call, misses show what they tried to say, and the other wrestler answers in their own voice. In 3,000 simulated promos, none play out silent.
- **Your wrestler always says their piece (v248).** The menu shows tactics, so the words behind a call used to appear only in how it went — and when a call missed, you'd often see just the reaction ("Name one.") to a line nobody heard. Now, when the result doesn't show your wrestler, they first deliver the line written for that call (or, for physical calls that miss, the attempt itself: "goes to jump them from behind"), then you see how it went. A few lines were also moved to the tactic they actually fit.
- **No meter (v247).** The tug-of-war bar is gone from promos. How your calls land still decides which closers you get, but there's no meter on screen and it no longer touches the score.
- **🎤 Promo showdown (v246).** Live promos play like a courtroom game: the camera whips between the two wrestlers (their real card photos on the card background, with face/heel name plates), each line types into a dialogue box with the speaker's name tab, and you tap to advance. Actions show in parentheses, the crowd and the room as gold camera notes, and anyone else who gets involved cuts in on the corner of the stage. Big moments get a shout across the stage (TAKE THAT! when a big-swing call lands, HOLD IT! when you cut someone off, LISTEN UP! when they wind up a big line, MIC DROP!), every call is stamped It lands or Not quite. Your calls take over the dialogue box when it's your turn. Party-night TV shows each line as it appears.
- **🎧 The promo exchange (v243).** Two-person Callouts, Champion's Addresses, Contract Signings and Sit-Down Interviews are a real back-and-forth now. After the opening, you call a tactic in your wrestler's ear (go personal, get in their face, play to or turn on the crowd, stay cool, make it about the match, show respect) and they find the words, written for what the other wrestler just said. Since v244 it's three decisions: the opening, one exchange call and the closer (a hot feud between two great talkers gets a second exchange call). How the exchange went decides the closer (mic drop when you're way ahead, a brawl, stare-down or match made when it's even, the other wrestler's last word when you're behind). Good talkers sometimes wind up a big line instead of answering, which you can cut off or let them finish. The extra calls are centred, so promos swing more without rating higher. The rival's promos play the same exchange.
- **🎬 Twists are occasional now (v233).** In a promo with a back-and-forth, your answer to the other wrestler usually closes the segment and counts as a full call. The old random twist (a run-in, the mic cutting out, a chant) only comes on top about a quarter of the time, so it's a surprise again. Crowd hijacks, the mystery figure and the countdown clock still come at their usual rate. Solo promos and turns keep their twist.
- **🔁 Back-and-forth promos (v228).** In a two-person Callout, Champion's Address, Contract Signing or Sit-Down Interview, the other wrestler fires back after your first call, and your next three options answer what they actually said. There are 230+ responses, picked by what you just did (insult, brag, promise, mind games, power play, challenge, handshake or respect, rough-up or a big attack) and whether it landed, the other wrestler's alignment and mic skill (weak talkers get short, blunt or tongue-tied lines; the sharpest ones need a great talker), titles on either side, feud heat, popularity gaps, a PPV coming up and the setting (no crowd lines in a studio). The response beat counts for a little less than a normal call, and the rival's promos get it too.
- **🎤 The mic drop.** When every call in a promo has landed, the twist can come with a fourth option: drop the mic for a guaranteed strong finish that ends the segment early, or push your luck for more.
- **👥 The crowd as a character.** On a finish (or a big moment in a main event, title match or PPV) where the crowd has a reason to lean — a hot feud, a big popularity gap, a title, a PPV — they sometimes make their wishes known: give them what they want for an extra ¼★, or go against them for heat. Rarely, in a live promo, they hijack it by chanting for someone on your roster who's more over than anyone in the ring: fight it, bring them out (a rub, a rivalry or a brand-new star) or cut to commercial. Never more than one crowd moment per show.
- **😤 Backstage confrontations.** Real backstage heat between two of your wrestlers can boil over between shows: separate them (a four-week truce — booking them together breaks it, and the Book screen warns you), let it simmer, or book them against each other that week, where the bad blood is guaranteed to come out in the match.
- **💬 Locker room whispers.** Now and then someone on your card pitches a specific spot for their match, based on their style, the stipulation and their alignment — tap it on the Book screen. Go for it and their match is guaranteed a call built around the idea; pass and they're a little let down. At most one a week.

## Morale, pitches & grievances
- **🚪 The line outside your office (v234, restyled v235).** Wrestlers with ideas, demands and requests, and every other out-of-the-ring call that comes with its own dialogue — front-office situations, injury decisions, tampering rumors, trade offers, countdown and open-challenge signing offers, prospect tapes and emails, teams in the making — line up outside your office at the start of the week. You see who's waiting and call them in one at a time ("2 of 3"); answer each to call in the next; then the briefing opens with everything else. Nothing goes live with one unanswered, and Home shows a strip if any are waiting.
- **Morale moves every week.** TV time, wins, main events, kept promises and answered pitches lift it. Sitting at home wears on people (stars after 2 weeks off TV, everyone else after 3), clean losses sting (more for stars, most when they lose to someone less popular), and being worn down or underpaid grinds on them. Tap any wrestler to see their **Mood** and the reasons behind it.
- **Pitches about every two weeks** (more often when the locker room is unhappy): main events, title shots, mic time, feuds, partners, rest, raises — plus **a spot on the next PPV** and **a dream match** with a bigger star.
- **Grievances escalate** when morale sinks, as front-office decisions you make before the next show: a **pay demand** (under 55), a **walkout threat** (under 45), a **shoot promo** on live TV or grievances at the **media scrum** (under 35), and an actual **walkout** for a few weeks (under 25, or after you call their bluff). Below 10, they may still demand their release.

## Tampering & injuries
- **🎧 Gorilla pitches.** Surprise run-ins and promo interruptions used to just happen and start rivalries on their own. Now the wrestler pitches the idea to you in gorilla first — same moments, no more often. Send them out and the scene plays as before; turn it down and the segment goes on as booked. It's ±2 morale either way.
- **🕵️ Tampering.** When an unhappy or underpaid wrestler is within 8 weeks of the end of their deal, the rival office (in a league, "another company") may call them. The line outside your office brings the rumor with the offer: match it and extend, negotiate, sit down with them, or let them go. Ignore it and, when the deal runs out, they sign with the rival on the spot (in a league they hit free agency). Re-sign them any way (the rumor's buttons, Negotiate, or the roster) and the rumor clears from the briefing right away.
- **🚑 Injury decisions.** When someone on your roster is hurt for a few weeks, choose: let it heal; rush them back in about half the time (fragile for 6 weeks after, with much higher re-injury risk); or, for longer injuries, surgery — more time out and a fee, but they come back tougher (+5 durability). Decisions wait in the briefing, with a card on Home.

- **Records** — most wins, title reigns, five-star matches, main events and PPV wins, the biggest legacies and the greatest matches.

## Trades
- Propose from the **Office** or the **🔁 Trade** button on another brand's wrestler: up to two wrestlers each way, plus cash either way. Champions can't be traded, and nobody can go over 30 wrestlers.
- **Solo:** the rival answers on the spot — accept, counter (they want more cash) or decline — based on each wrestler's overall, upside and contract length. On Hard it drives a harder bargain. Every few weeks the rival may call with an offer of its own; it waits in your briefing for a week.
- **Leagues:** offers wait in the other GM's briefing until they accept or decline (or you withdraw). Offers expire after 3 weeks.
- Traded wrestlers keep their contracts. Happy ones take it a little hard; unhappy ones welcome the fresh start.

## The road to the PPV
- Every PPV has a **hype meter** (Home and the Book screen) that builds over the weeks before it: good TV shows add hype and flat ones cost some, hot feuds you keep going add a little, and **announced PPV matches** (Contract Signings, or callouts booked for the PPV) add a lot — up to four.
- **🎟️ The title picture** (top of the Titles screen) lists every title shot you owe — Rumble, tournament and Casino Gauntlet winners, title-shot pitches you agree to ("win two and you're next" makes them the #1 contender), contract clauses — with the date and the feud heat against the champion. Earning a shot starts the rivalry (🔥15). Build it past the gold line (🔥35) before the night: inside 4 weeks the Book screen checklist and the weekly briefing remind you, **🎤 Build it** books a face-to-face promo, and Suggest the rest gives them a promo slot. If the belt changes hands first, the shot follows it. The Home screen's Titles tile shows how many shots you owe and how many are behind.
- The **go-home show** right before the PPV matters most: a strong one adds a lot, a flat one costs you.
- On the night, hype moves **ticket sales** (−15% to +15%) and the crowd: a hot build brings extra fans, a cold one costs some. Then the meter resets.

## Party night (same room, big screen)
Party night is a way to play an online league, not a separate game. It uses the same league, the same save and the same code, so you can play some weeks together on the couch and the rest on your own time.

- **Start it:** any GM opens the league's **Office → Party night → Start party night**. Everyone in the league now books **at the same time** instead of taking turns. That covers creating wrestlers too. The draft still goes pick by pick.
- **Lock in, then go live on the TV:** book your card and tap **Lock in my card**. You can unlock it until everyone else is in. Once every card is locked, GMs go live **one at a time on the TV**. Your phone says "You're live!" and you play your show there, but every promo beat, match call, choice and outcome is shown on the TV so the room can watch and shout advice. When the last GM wraps up, the week airs and every phone gets its results.
- **Picks on the TV:** when the live GM taps a promo or match choice, the TV lights up that option for a moment, then shows how it played out with a "Picked" tag on top. Your phone does the same on every show, party night or not: the call you tap lights up with a ✓ Picked tag while the others dim, and the outcome keeps a "You picked" strip on top.
- **The TV:** on any screen, open the game, tap **Show a league on this screen** and enter the league code. The TV follows the league live. It shows who's still booking, the draft board, the standings and whoever is live right now (choices and all). Then it plays each week's shows side by side, segment by segment, alternating between the two shows: stars sweep in, the better segment in each slot gets ▲ Wins the round, and the final ratings fill in before the winner is announced. It doesn't take a seat, and it can **Replay last week**. It fills any size screen: a PC running a 4K TV at 100% Windows scaling gets the same layout as 1080p, just sharper (150% and 200% scaling look the same too). If the league still has open seats, the TV shows a QR code so friends can join.
- **The locker-room feed:** while everyone books, the TV plays each office visit the moment a GM answers it. The ring bell rings, the wrestler's own line comes up, and the GM's answer is stamped underneath ("✓ Maria says yes", "✕ Josh says no"). That GM's tile shows **🚪 In the office** while theirs is up, and answered visits fold into a short list underneath. Visits that are strategy (rival tampering, the trainer, trade offers and prospect tapes) are only teased: the room sees who got the call, never the money, the weeks out or the trade.
- **Sound on the TV:** on party night the TV plays the sounds for the room and the phones stay quiet. Tap **🔊** in the TV header to switch them off and on (after a reload, the TV needs one tap anywhere before it can make sound).
- **Everyone at once:** phones save at the same time, and each save is merged into the league. If two GMs grab the same thing in the same moment (say, the same free agent), the first one gets it and the other phone refreshes — keeping that GM's card, lock-in and private plans. Two GMs forming different tag teams or factions at once both stick.
- **End it:** **Office → End party night** (or the link in the banner). The league goes back to taking turns from exactly where it is. GMs who already went live that week stay locked in, and everyone else goes live on their own turn. If someone has to leave early, end party night and they book their show later with turn alerts as usual.
- Needs the Firebase setup in [ONLINE-SETUP.md](ONLINE-SETUP.md) and an internet connection on every device. No new setup beyond online leagues.

## Your saves
Turn on **Office → Cloud save** to back up automatically and continue on any device with your sync code (needs the one-time Firebase setup in [ONLINE-SETUP.md](ONLINE-SETUP.md)). The game also keeps automatic backups of your last few weeks under **Office → Backups**.

- **Every solo game syncs both ways.** Each game has its own slot under your sync code. Enter the code on another device and all your games appear in ⇄ Your games there; play any of them anywhere and the others catch up.
- **Saving stays safe.** Everything saves on the device first and the cloud catches up in the background (offline play queues). An upload only lands if nobody else saved that game since this device last synced it, so nothing is overwritten blindly. If both devices played the same game before syncing, you're asked which save to keep — never decided for you — and the other is kept as a backup. A newer cloud save never replaces unsynced play on this device, the game you're playing gets a backup first, and nothing swaps while you're mid-show or mid-edit.
- **Deleting is reversible.** Deleting a game removes it from your other devices, but its cloud copy stays: **Backups → Deleted games** brings it back everywhere. A device that played a game after it was deleted elsewhere keeps it and puts it back.
- **Updating from v195 or earlier** is automatic: the game cloud save used to follow keeps one shared id on every device, games that already had a cloud copy are matched up instead of uploaded twice, and a device still on an older version keeps syncing until it updates. No Firebase rule changes are needed.

Saves and photos live on each device, inside the installed app. The installed app does **not** share saves with a copy opened from Files or a browser tab, so use **Office → Export** in the old copy and **Import** in the new one to move a season over (and **Export Photo Pack / Import Photo Pack** for pictures). Exporting now and then is a good backup.
