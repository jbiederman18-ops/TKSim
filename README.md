# Tony Khan Simulator

A wrestling general-manager game: draft from AEW and WWE's rosters, book Dynamite and Collision through a full year — a PPV every 4 weeks, alternating AEW and WWE's biggest shows from the Royal Rumble through All In to Survivor Series — and win the fan war. Works fully offline once installed.

## Put it on GitHub Pages
1. Create a new public repository on GitHub (e.g. `all-elite-gm`).
2. Upload every file in this folder to the repository root (**Add file → Upload files**).
3. Go to **Settings → Pages**, set **Source** to *Deploy from a branch*, choose `main` and `/ (root)`, and save.
4. After a minute your game is live at `https://<your-username>.github.io/all-elite-gm/`.

## Install on your phone
- **iPhone (Safari):** open the link, tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**.
- **Android (Chrome):** open the link, tap **⋮ → Install app** (or *Add to Home screen*).

Open it once while online; after that it launches with no connection.

## Updating the game
Upload the changed files (usually `index.html`, plus any `.js` files that changed, such as `season.js` or `tutorial.js`), then open `sw.js` and bump the version in `VERSION` (e.g. `aegm-v36` → `aegm-v37`) so phones pick up the update. Installed copies update the next time they're opened online (close and reopen the app once).

## Play online with a friend
Host a league for 2–4 GMs and friends join with a 6-letter code. Everyone runs their own brand — with 2 GMs it's head to head every week, and with 3 or 4 every show competes each week and the night is ranked — and the most fans gained by Survivor Series wins. Everyone creates wrestlers and books each week at the same time (the week airs when the last GM locks in); the draft goes in order, and you can queue up to 10 picks so they're made for you the moment you're on the clock. Offline changes sync later, and each player gets a notification when it's their move. One-time setup: see [ONLINE-SETUP.md](ONLINE-SETUP.md).

## New players: the tour
A new game opens with **X Redditino**, your Head of PR, waking you up on draft day with what's at stake. Then comes a one-minute **draft tour** (it starts on its own; Skip tour is on every stop), then a short **first-week tour** once the draft is done: your show and how you win, Needs your attention, Suggest the rest, booking by feel, feuds and going live. Everything else is a one-time **coach tip** that pops up the first time it matters — match details, your first results, PPV week, the AEW/WWE calendar, the running order, your crowds, stamina, the Roster, Free agents, Titles and Office screens — at most one booking tip a week. Replay it any time from **⚙️ → Rookie orientation** (where you can also turn coach tips on or off).

During the draft, every pick the rival (or another GM) makes slides in at the top of the screen — **The pick is in**: round and pick, who took them, their photo, Ring / Mic / Pop, crowd and salary. Tap it to clear it; picks that land together queue up.

Under the round counter, a row of tiles counts your men (M), women (W) and wrestlers for each crowd (a two-crowd wrestler counts for both); a tile lit red is a crowd you're behind on for this point in the draft. Casual stars draw casuals (up to three in matches a show count), so the draft aims for about six Casuals and about four of each other crowd. Your cap sits under it as one line — what's left and about how much that is per pick — and each team on the **Draft board** shows its own tiles. **Auto-pick**, **Sim rest** and **Auto-draft me** draft for the crowds you're short on and wrestlers who click with the ones you have, alongside talent and price; Auto-pick tells you why it made its pick. Photo Studio is in the ⚙️ Game menu.

The tour reads its numbers straight from the game and is checked automatically on every update (`tools/check-tutorial.py`), so it keeps up as the rules change. When a stop changes, players who already took the tour get a short **What's new** replay of just those stops.

## AEW meets WWE
- **One talent pool.** WWE's Raw and SmackDown rosters (current to Money in the Bank, Oct 10, 2026) are in the draft and free agency alongside AEW's. Ratings reflect each wrestler's current ability and popularity on the same scale — WWE's biggest names are the most popular wrestlers in the game. Real WWE tag teams and factions come together (The Bloodline, The Vision, Judgment Day, LWO, The Lucha Bros…), and anyone currently hurt or on leave starts out injured in an existing save.
- **Supplemental draft for older saves.** A save that started before the WWE update can hold a one-time WWE Supplemental Draft from the Home screen: 3 rounds, only the unsigned WWE wrestlers, the brand behind in the fan war picks first, and a budget on top of each brand's payroll with room for two big stars and a solid third pick. It runs on the normal draft screen and drops back into the same week; anyone not picked stays a free agent. You can skip it instead.
- **Photos.** 🔍 Search for a WWE wrestler looks on wwe.com for their official photo; in **Quick photo mode**, tap **WWE** to run through just the WWE roster.
- **A bigger draft budget.** The draft salary cap is 20% higher than before (about $527K/wk in a two-GM game), so each brand can afford more top stars for star-vs-star matches. Revenue grows only half as much, so the extra payroll is a real cost and the bank stays tighter.
- **WWE's world titles** — Undisputed WWE, World Heavyweight, WWE Women's and WWE Women's World — join the ten AEW belts, so each show starts with seven.
- **Two supershows.** WrestleMania (week 12) and All In (week 24) are the climaxes of the WWE and AEW halves of the year: hotter crowds, double the fan swing, bigger gates and bigger venue bills.
- **The calendar** alternates the biggest shows: Royal Rumble, Revolution, WrestleMania, Double or Nothing, Money in the Bank, All In, SummerSlam, All Out, Full Gear and Survivor Series, which closes the year. The Owen Hart Cups now end at Double or Nothing and the Continental Classic at Full Gear.
- **🏟️ Royal Rumble.** A men's (8) and a women's (6) Rumble lock onto your Royal Rumble card, which runs five matches in all (Money in the Bank runs six). You pick the entrants (Clear entrants empties the field), the winner and who's eliminated last — the runner-up gets a small popularity bump instead of a loss. The winner earns a world title shot at WrestleMania against your best champion in their division (belts never leave their show, so if yours are vacant the winner is first in line when one is crowned).
- **💼 Money in the Bank.** A men's (6) and a women's (5) ladder match lock onto your Money in the Bank card. The winner holds the briefcase for a year and can cash in on a world champion of their division on your brand at any time: book them against the champion and make it the cash-in, or open any match the champion is in and have the holder cash in straight after it. You decide whether the cash-in works; it lifts the match it follows (+½★ when it succeeds). Unused briefcases expire at the next Money in the Bank, and the rival cashes in too.
- **Survivor Series is the crossover show** (it took Forbidden Door's place): up to two dream matches against the rival's stars, and rival invasions in the TV weeks before it.
- **Existing saves** keep their week: the WWE wrestlers arrive in free agency, WWE's titles start vacant (two for each brand), and the PPVs take their new names from here on.

## A wrestler's profile
Tap any wrestler (hold a card on a phone, right-click it on a computer) to open their profile. Their Ring, Mic and Pop are the three big numbers up top, the best one in white. Below that, in plain lists:
- **Needs you** (only when something does): a walkout threat, a debut, main events, title shots and promised matches they're owed, and a **New deal** button in a contract's last weeks.
- **Right now** — stamina, morale and what's moving it (including "Needs TV this week" or "Wants the spotlight" before it costs you), a chip on their shoulder, backstage friends, durability and their **📖 Arc** (start one here, or see its progress).
- **Their crowd** — the fan group they draw and what it wants, plus the stipulation they're a natural in once you've found it.
- **Feuds** by tier (Heat, Rivalry, Feud, Blood feud); tap one for its story.
- **Best matchups** and tag partners on your roster (tap a name to see why), then traits, contract and record, and a folded-up career.

Someone else's wrestler leads with what you'd do about them: draft, sign, make an offer or trade.

## Playing on a phone
- **Go live** stays pinned just above the tabs on Book, with what's left to book and fix beside it — no scrolling to the bottom to air the show. It steps aside while the pick sheet is open.
- **Swipe a sheet down** by its handle to close it (the same as tapping the dimmed area above it). Sheets you have to answer — a visitor at your office door, a showdown — have no handle.
- **The back button** (or back swipe) closes the open sheet, then the pick sheet, then takes you back to the screen you were on, then Home. On Home with nothing open, back leaves the app as usual. It does nothing mid-show, mid-showdown or with someone at your office door.
- Small buttons (the week chip, ⚙️, Details, finish and run-in tags, names in This week and on Titles) have bigger touch areas, and the smallest card text (stamina, R/M/P) has a minimum size.

## Playing on a computer
On a screen 1100px or wider (never on the party-night TV) the game lays itself out for a desktop: the tabs move up into the header, Home, the Office and Settings run in two columns, sheets open as centred windows, and the booking pick sheet docks down the right side so the card table stays in view — click another slot on the table to switch to it.
- **Mouse:** everything clickable lights up on hover. Right-click a wrestler card for their details (the same as holding it on a phone). The scroll wheel scrolls sideways rows without Shift.
- **Keyboard:** **1–6** switch tabs (Home, Book, Roster, Arcs, Titles, Office) and **0** opens Settings. **A–E** answer a lettered choice (the office line, promo and match showdowns, live calls). **Space**, **Enter** or **→** advance showdowns and the star reveal. **Esc** closes the open sheet (never mid-showdown or mid-reveal) or the pick sheet. **Tab** reaches every clickable panel; **Enter** or **Space** presses it, and **I** on a wrestler card opens their details. In the tour, **←/→** step through and **Esc** skips.
- **The screens:** a **Go live** button sits in the header on every screen (**G** presses it) — on Book it's the real Go live, anywhere else it takes you to Book. Book runs in two panes: the card on the left, everything about the show's health (Needs your attention, Go live, your building, vacant titles, Production, the bill, feuds, the locker room) in a panel on the right that stays in view; it steps aside while the pick sheet is open. The roster has a **Cards / Table** switch — the table sorts by any column. In wrestler details opened from the roster or the draft, **‹ ›** or **←/→** step through the list. Hover a card's stat line or condition for the full numbers, or a Wild card tag for what it means. Home drops the tiles that repeat the header tabs, and in the draft the Tale of the tape stays pinned beside the board.

## The countdown

Now and then a countdown clock appears during a solo promo. Three weeks later a free agent from the other side of the face/heel line debuts and goes after the wrestler who had the mic (instant heat), and you get one week to sign them at a discount — bigger if the promo hyped the clock. Sign them early, or let the rival, and they debut for whoever did.

## How wrestlers grow
- **Anyone can become a megastar.** Every wrestler grows on the same curve, so anyone can be built into a megastar. Raw talent learns fast; In-ring and Mic gains slow past 80 and crawl past 90. Working matches (faster against better opponents and in better matches), promos for Mic, and arcs all feed it. A wrestler's profile says where they are: Learning fast, Growing, Gains slowing, Elite or Rare air.
- **The top is hard to hold.** In-ring of 85+ slips a little in any week without a strong enough match (3★ through the low 90s, about 3½★ at 95) or an In-ring arc running.
- **Popularity at the top takes upkeep.** Stars at 85+ slip a little in any week without a main event, a PPV win or a 3½★+ match (half as much if they carried a lower name in a decent multi-person match). At 90+ only a main event, a PPV win or a 4★+ match holds their spot, so keeping an ace on top means keeping them in your biggest matches.
- **Hidden learning speed.** Everyone has a secret speed for In-ring and for Mic (most near normal, a few much faster or slower), rolled fresh every new game, so the same roster develops differently each time. Work with someone for a while and their profile shows the extremes: 📈 Quick study or 🐢 Slow learner.
- **Chemistry changes every game.** Traits (Monster, Underdog, Daredevil…) are dealt fresh each new game, still in character, and they drive a lot of chemistry. Pairs who've never met still roll their spark the first time they share a ring.
- **Rookie tiers** (Prospect, Blue-Chip, Generational) set how polished a rookie starts and what they cost, not how good they can get.
- **The offline rival** only trains wrestlers below 72 in-ring, and its stars stop climbing at 89+ (no upkeep slip for them either). Leagues have no offline rival, so every GM grows the same way.

## Scouting & ROH (the farm system)
- **Three scouting boards** (Roster → Free agents → Scouting), refreshing every few weeks: **🌱 Indies** (raw prospects and gimmick acts, the cheapest and longest build), **🏟️ ROH** (polished regulars plus the real ROH roster — Jay Lethal, Matt Sydal, Top Flight, the Von Erichs, Premier Athletes, Dalton Castle, Lacey Lane and more. They aren't in the AEW draft, so ROH is the only place to get them. Ready sooner, less upside) and **🌏 Overseas** (skilled imports from Japan, Mexico and the UK who are still learning promos).
- **Known names from the PWI 500.** Every Indies and Overseas board also carries one or two real names from outside AEW and WWE — men ranked on the 2026 PWI 500 (Leon Slater, Nic Nemeth, Mustafa Ali, Michael Oku, Yota Tsuji, Zack Sabre Jr., Templario, Atlantis Jr. and more, with their rank on the card) plus top independent and joshi women (Masha Slamovich, Sareee, Saya Kamitani, Starlight Kid, Lady Flammer…). They cost more than prospects but arrive closer to ready.
- **ROH books itself every week.** Everyone on your ROH roster works a match on HonorClub against ROH regulars or other GMs' prospects, builds an ROH record and keeps growing. ROH's four titles (World, Women's World, TV, Pure) are defended every other week by the best records. Wins, good matches and ROH gold build **call-up buzz** (🧊 → 🔥), which fades if they cool off. When someone turns hot (9+ buzz — in practice an ROH title run, or winning nearly every week for a couple of months), they come to your office door asking for the call-up (call them up, or not yet — they ask again in 4 weeks if they're still hot) — and the hotter they are, the bigger their AEW debut. The ROH screen shows the champions, last week's results, the standings and your watchlist.
- **Tapes & emails.** Some weeks a prospect sends you their tape or an email (up to 2 waiting, each for 3 weeks) — it lands in the line outside your office at the start of the week. Pass, **keep following** them (up to 6 — they send updates as they improve, get a little pricier, and may sign elsewhere), or sign them straight to ROH.
- **Sign to AEW or to ROH.** AEW signings take a roster spot and can be booked right away. ROH signings go to your ROH roster (Roster → 🏟️ ROH, up to 8): no AEW roster spot, half salary, and they keep growing every week on ROH TV, slower than the Performance Center.
- **Feature** one ROH wrestler to grow faster and build popularity (up to 45). Send someone on a once-per-career **10-week excursion**: NJPW (biggest in-ring jump and a tougher body), CMLL (comes back a bigger draw) or RevPro (real mic reps). They come back sharper (extra in-ring and mic) with a bigger debut pop.
- **Rebrands.** Calling someone up, or signing a prospect to ROH, opens a quick fresh-start sheet first: keep them as they are, or give them a new ring name (type one or tap an idea) and start them as a face or a heel. Past news keeps the old name.
- **Call up** anyone whenever you need them (they get a debut pop), and **send down** rookies or anyone at 45 popularity or lower to develop. Champions can't be sent down. ROH deals run out like any contract — extend them from the ROH screen.
- **Teams reunite.** Sign or call up both halves of a real ROH team (Top Flight, the Von Erichs, Premier Athletes…) and they're a team again on your roster.
- Works in online leagues too: every GM has their own ROH roster.

## Offers & contracts
- **Free agents with 55+ popularity want an offer**, not just a fee: a signing bonus, a weekly salary, and promises — 🎬 creative control, ⭐ main event spots, 🏆 a title shot, 📺 a place on every PPV. Each wrestler wants different things (a big star wants control, a hungry midcarder wants main events), and the offer screen tells you how it looks to them. Smaller names still sign on the spot.
- **Sealed bids.** Solo, the rival may send an offer too and the wrestler picks right away. In a league, every other GM can send one sealed counter before the week airs, and the wrestler picks the best package when it does. On party night the TV reveals every offer in a "Signing war" before the results.
- **Contract countdown.** Deals with 5 weeks or less left (9 for stars) show on a ✍️ card on Home, amber at 3 weeks and red on the last show. On the last show the agent comes to your office door — two shows out for anyone at 55+ popularity or holding a title (since v341; every contract's last two shows from v324): talk now, put it off a week, or on the last show let them test the market. The Book screen repeats it, and going live on someone's last show asks you to re-sign them or let them go first
- **Re-signing is a negotiation** (from the agent at your office door, Home's contract countdown or the wrestler's card). They make demands; agree to some or all, or counter on money. They take it, counter back (up to three rounds) or walk away, depending on their morale, how hot your show is and whether you've kept your word before. Since v315: refuse a demand and the money options go past their ask, so you can buy it out; a lowball can insult anyone ("Could insult them" in the read of the room) and they walk; and their counter digs in on the demand they care about most.
- **Promises are contract terms.** The Book screen shows what's owed. Keep one and morale rises (and a little lost trust comes back); break one and morale drops; after three, a wrestler may walk out. An injury pauses the clock on a promise. Only true main eventers ask for creative control, it lasts 12 weeks, and a roster can have at most 3 people with it. **Creative control** means they won't agree to lose. Booked to lose, they show on the checklist with **Talk to them** (and again at go-live): offer to protect them in the finish (a cheat, an upset roll-up or a count-out instead of a clean pin), a big win down the line (a main-event or PPV win within 4 weeks), or their win back (beat the same opponent by the next PPV). Each is easier to sell than the last; morale, broken promises, how hot your show is and who they're losing to all count. Go live without a deal and about half the time they change the finish and go over (−¼★); otherwise they do the job under protest and morale drops hard. A promised win stays on the checklist until it happens — miss the deadline and it's a broken promise.

## Booking by feel
- **🎤 Talk and wrestle on the same show (v392).** Cutting a promo never cost stamina, and the booking sheet now treats it that way: a promo slot only sets aside people already cutting a promo tonight, and a match slot only people already in a match, so a wrestler in a match isn't buried under Already booked when you pick a promo (they get a **Wrestles later** tab, and **Hypes their match** once you pick someone from across the ring). A promo earlier in the night between wrestlers from two different sides of a match builds it for later: that match gets about a quarter star. A promo with someone unrelated, or one that airs after the match, doesn't count. It shows in the crowd buzz, Show flow, Details (Story) and Why that rating?. The Details pips also read the right ingredients again: they'd been reading the wrong rows since morale and locker-room mood were added.
- **🏆 New champions stay in their box (v391).** In the star reveal (and the party TV's reveal), the "And new…" title moment now plays inside that match's own box instead of taking over the whole screen. Taps go straight through to the reveal, and it clears itself after a few seconds.
- **🧹 No more rundown (v381).** The "Meanwhile on the card" sheet before a highlight reel, and the TV's end-of-turn rundown, are gone — they repeated what the star reveal shows. Office's Contracts expiring list shows small card thumbnails again instead of full-size cards.
- **🧾 Style steps back in the draft (v357).** Wrestler cards on the draft, free-agent and roster grids no longer list a style, and neither does the Tale of the tape. Ring, Mic and Pop are what make a pick. Style is still on every wrestler's detail screen (with who they click with), as a small note on the draft board and the TV's "Just picked" card, and as a filter under **More filters**. It only matters for who you pair them with, and that shows when you book.
- **☁️ Sign in with Google (v383).** Cloud save now has an account: **⚙️ Game menu → Saves & backups → Sign in with Google**, then sign in on any other device and every game shows up there, no code to type. Your sync code still works as a backup, a device already on a different code is moved onto your account without replacing anything, and signing in or out never touches your league seats.
- **📺 The network brings the check (v356).** The viewership bonus isn't paid silently with the TV deal any more. It builds up every week your audience is bigger than the deal was signed for, and the week after each PPV the network's executive comes to your office with the check: one visit, no decision, the money goes into the bank. What they say depends on how hot your recent shows are and how much your audience has grown. Finances shows the weekly bonus and what the network owes you so far. The offline rival still gets its bonus weekly.
- **💰 The draft is a money choice (v355).** New games start with **$350K** (the offline rival keeps $1M). A new company's TV deal starts smaller and grows faster with your audience (about the old deal at 700K fans, +40% at 1M), and during the season the network adds a **📈 viewership bonus** as your audience grows — so money is tight in the first weeks and growing the brand pays right away instead of only next season. Drafted contracts now run 16–52 weeks instead of 14–40, so season 1's re-signings are spread out instead of all landing in the back half (which used to put 20–40% of games in the red after week 17). In the sweep, drafting to the cap dips the bank to about $250K by week 3–6 and recovers as fans grow; leaving 15–30% of the cap open banks $700K–1M to buy free agents with; both end season 1 with similar results. Existing saves keep their bank and contracts.
- **📺 Party-night TV catch-up (v351).** Most shows have no highlight reel now, so a GM's live turn used to flash past on the TV with nothing to watch. Now the TV runs **the rundown** of every segment that played quietly before that GM wraps up ("No highlight reels tonight" when none did). The booking-night poster on the TV no longer shows the first GM's (secret) main event or their Hottest right now strip, which also kept the locker-room feed from falling off the bottom of the screen. The live sheet comes back to full size for each new segment instead of shrinking all night, a match result spans the whole TV, the draft board shows Ring / Mic / Pop, the season-over screen shows the year's awards, each locked-in GM's face-down pile counts the segments they actually booked, and a league on the TV with party night off no longer says GMs take turns. *(Dropped in v381 — the reveal shows the same thing.)*
- **📈 The flow graph matches the rating (v350).** The Show flow graph now draws from exactly the segments the projected show rating uses, so crowd chants and a chair stashed under the ring count, and matches with an injured wrestler drop out. Every structure penalty shows on it: a sagging back half and a long run of matches with no story segment turn those bars red too, and a show with nothing for the women's division says so under the graph. Tutorial tips that changed recently come up once more for players who'd already seen them, and a rebrand can't reuse any name in the game (ROH rosters and scouting boards included).
- **📈 Show flow graph (v348).** Under the running order, every segment is a bar (its crowd-buzz band) set against a dashed line: the shape the show rating rewards for that card — a strong opener, a middle that can dip a little and then builds, and a main event that tops every other match. A bar turns red when the rating is docking the show for it (a flat opener, a dead spot, an outshone main event, back-to-back promos); tap a bar for one line on what it is and why. The verdict and red bars are worked out from the same projections as the live show's rating, so the preview no longer flags things that won't happen. A **rub match** — a star working someone well below them — never counts as a dead spot or a flat opener, and in tags, trios, 8-man tags, triple threats and four-ways the best workers now carry a weaker one instead of the weakest worker dragging the whole match down.
- **Non-title tag and fewer obvious hints (v389).** Any match with a champion in it and no title on the line shows a **Non-title** tag that opens the Title tab — including intergender and other matches where the belt can't go up, which now say why in one line. Another pass removed hints that stated the obvious (the inaugural champions blurb, "tap to view or edit", "optional, for quick cash" and similar).
- **Leaner helper text (v388).** About a third of the explanatory copy is gone: party night, online leagues, squashes and created wrestlers are each explained once, and long paragraphs (Photo Studio, ROH, Solo TV, After Dark, the draft queue, sealed bids) are a line or two. The old match and promo editors are retired; a booked slot's **Details** opens a read-only breakdown with **Change it** back to the sheet, and the style chart lives on every wrestler's page under **How chemistry works**. Leagues always book at the same time (the Take turns option is gone), and the league standings now name the right season finale.
- **Every show gets the full set (v387).** Each show now has a men's and women's world, midcard and tag title (eight belts each). New: WWE Tag Team, WWE Women's Tag Team and Women's United States; the National title is retired. Older saves get the new belts vacant on whichever show was missing them; a National title someone still holds stays.
- **Titles stay in their division (v386).** Only wrestlers from a belt's division (men's or women's) can compete for it — the Title tab won't offer a belt for an intergender match. Intergender matches are still welcome as non-title bouts. A cross-division champion in an older save can still defend until the belt goes back.
- **Heat tags match the feud meter (v385).** A card's feud tab uses the same names as the feud tiers: **Heat** (just getting started), **🔥 Rivalry**, **🔥🔥 Feud** and **🔥🔥🔥 Blood feud** — no more "Feud" meaning less than "Rivals".
- **The whole roster, best fit first (v384).** Tapping a space shows everyone on your roster in best-fit order (Needs you tonight on top, Already booked at the bottom — for a promo, only people already cutting a promo tonight; for a match, only people already in a match, v392) instead of a short Best fits list; Champions, Faces and Heels narrow it in the same order. Rivals now count against everyone across the ring: in a tag, trios or multi-way match, anyone feuding with any wrestler on another side leads the list, tagged with who they're feuding with.
- **Rivals lead the best fits (v341).** Once one side of a match or promo is picked, their rivals and feuds sit at the top of Best fits (hottest first), however tired they are; rating and chemistry order everyone after them. Not when you're picking a tag partner.
- **Risk tells match the swing (v341).** A match showdown call only reads **Safe** with good odds and a small swing; a medium or big swing reads Risky at best, a huge one Playing with fire.

An opening that ends the segment now ends it: a contract signing that breaks into a pull-apart brawl, a table flipped into a pull-apart brawl, a fight called down to the ring, a walk-out, a chair shot followed by walking off — no more answers, exchanges or crowd moments after the locker room has cleared the ring (the rival's segments follow the same rule). A twist that takes the closer's place after a back-and-forth is now one that fits a confrontation with the other wrestler standing right there (security, a run-in, the other wrestler's music, the mic cutting out) — never "You deserve it!", a kid in a costume or a sing-along. Sneak attacks get their own aftermath twists, and while the target is down nothing needs them up and talking. Once the match is made — a contract on the table or a challenge already accepted — nobody asks for a yes again, and after a landed handshake or show of respect the closer stays respectful (no boil-over or knife-twist; a new "Shake on it"). Studio and backstage twists no longer mention the ring or the crowd, a faction rally isn't interrupted by the faction it's rallying, and twists where the other wrestler walks out only come when they weren't already standing there. In the main event, the big moment is about the same two people the showdown shows (no pinning one opponent while the moment kicks out the other), the booth doesn't cheer an injury stoppage, and tapping while the camera is still moving no longer skips a line, in matches or promos.
- **Main event calls only, and a match decision sweep (v255).** Undercard matches no longer stop for a random call, so a show moves faster: only the main event showdown has calls. Undercard matches still stop when something forces it — a hurt, gassed or unhappy wrestler, a grudge you told them to settle in the ring, or a whisper you approved — and the rival's undercard follows the same rule. Every match moment was swept for how it fits the showdown: each call's outcome lines were read and the ones that end the match (a three count, a tap, a bell, a ten count, a DQ, a prize pulled down…) are now known to the game. A moment plays in the heat only with calls that keep the match going; a moment whose calls end it is saved for the finish, and a finish call that keeps it going is followed by the winner putting it away. General finishes (a ref stoppage, a slow count) only end pinfall and submission matches, and a moment whose setup already ended it never gets a second finish. Moment lines now page by sentence with the camera on whoever starts the sentence (no more misread quotes), the intro names both teams (and calls the Rumble, Gauntlet and Money in the Bank by name), it only talks about bad blood when there is some, the near-fall call shows its swing, and the read-the-crowd tags are re-worked for the calls you actually get.
- **🎬 Match showdown (v254).** A match highlight reel (the main event, before v260) plays like the promo showdown: both sides on the stage with the camera whipping between them, a commentary booth (play-by-play and colour) calling it, shouts across the screen (OH MY GOD!, KICKED OUT!, THREE!) and a count stamp on the cover. You call it in three acts: the **opening** tactic (fly out of the gate, chain wrestle, start a fight, throw them around, play to the crowd, a face flurry or a heel jumping the opponent at the bell — the one that fits your winner's style comes first and has better odds), the **heat** (the match's big moment — the same moments, situations and disasters as before, always one in the main event; when that moment is a finish, it's saved for the end and the heat gets a tactic call instead), the **near fall** (kick out at 2.9 for more drama and a bigger finish at +3 stamina for everyone, or stay down and end it there); since v302, a kickout that lands in a pinfall match lets you **push your luck** with another near fall, and another — each is riskier (**Safe / Risky / Playing with fire**), more tiring and swings harder, the crowd peaks at two or three kickouts and then tires of them, and a miss gets worse the deeper you went (sloppy kickout → ref miscount → blown finish that ends it on the spot), with its own ±1.5★ budget and the **finish** (only finishes that match allows: covers and roll-ups, rope breaks and taps, "I quit", ladders, tables, ten counts, coffins, Blood & Guts surrenders, Rumble eliminations). After the bell you pick the closing shot (celebration, handshake, a post-match beatdown or a heel jumping the winner, a staredown) — it moves heat and popularity, not stars. New calls swing by their odds like every other call, scaled down because there are more of them, and together never move the match more than ¾★ either way (v300: 1½★ — they're rarer and swing about 2½× as hard; v301: a miss costs what a hit would have won). The rival's main event runs the same calls by the rival's rules. It all mirrors to party-night TV.
- **Promo flow sweep (v253).** Every line in a back-and-forth was checked against the calls that can follow it, and every call against the answers it can get. Answers now fit what actually happened: a shove gets a shove-sized reaction (no bloody lip from a mic to the chest), a stare-down line never follows a laugh or a yawn, "nice speech" never follows a silent face-off, a dodge or "maybe next week" never follows a challenge that landed, and nobody warmly thanks you after leaving your hand hanging. Walk-away comebacks only show when the other wrestler is actually walking away, a wrestler laid out on the floor ends the segment instead of trading lines, studio-only lines (chairs, tables) stay in the studio, and a backstage callout gets its own laid-out, boil-over and hold-the-moment versions. When the other wrestler's big line lands, you answer that line instead of them getting a second one on top of it. A few new answers fill gaps (a belt in the challenger's face, a brawl security stops, a refused handshake, studio fights).
- **Promos that follow (v250).** A full sweep of how promo lines lead into each other. Nobody answers a wrestler who has walked off: when a call ends with someone leaving (your wrestler storms out, the other one slips away and you don't stop them) or flat on the mat, the exchange ends there instead of carrying on with a reply and a closer, and a twist never needs someone who's gone. Answers fit what was actually said — a handshake that already happened isn't refused, a demand to kneel gets told where to go, fine print signed unread gets a shrug, a counter after a missed attack doesn't replay the counter, tag partners don't get rival-only twists, and a line is never echoed straight back. On screen, lines go to the right speaker (accented names, "A and B" moments, "{A}'s music hits", dashes around a quote, the announcer or a fan speaking), reactions play out in full instead of being cut to their first sentence, and the other wrestler no longer gets a second last word right after landing their big line.
- **Every promo is spoken (v249).** About 2,600 outcomes used to be narration only ("A gritty training montage. They look ready for war.") — in rallies, attacks, vignettes, turns and mid-promo twists, roughly a third of promos had nobody say a word. Every one now has dialogue: your wrestler speaks on every landed call, misses show what they tried to say, and the other wrestler answers in their own voice. In 3,000 simulated promos, none play out silent.
- **Your wrestler always says their piece (v248).** The menu shows tactics, so the words behind a call used to appear only in how it went — and when a call missed, you'd often see just the reaction ("Name one.") to a line nobody heard. Now, when the result doesn't show your wrestler, they first deliver the line written for that call (or, for physical calls that miss, the attempt itself: "goes to jump them from behind"), then you see how it went. A few lines were also moved to the tactic they actually fit.
- **No meter (v247).** The tug-of-war bar is gone from promos. How your calls land still decides which closers you get, but there's no meter on screen and it no longer touches the score.
- **🎤 Promo showdown (v246).** Live promos play like a courtroom game: the camera whips between the two wrestlers (their real card photos on the card background, with face/heel name plates), each line types into a dialogue box with the speaker's name tab, and you tap to advance. Actions show in parentheses, the crowd and the room as gold camera notes, and anyone else who gets involved cuts in on the corner of the stage. Big moments get a shout across the stage (TAKE THAT! when a big-swing call lands, HOLD IT! when you cut someone off, LISTEN UP! when they wind up a big line, MIC DROP!), every call is stamped It lands or Not quite. Your calls take over the dialogue box when it's your turn. Party-night TV shows each line as it appears.
- **🎧 The promo exchange (v243).** Two-person Callouts, Champion's Addresses, Contract Signings and Sit-Down Interviews are a real back-and-forth now. After the opening, you call a tactic in your wrestler's ear (go personal, get in their face, play to or turn on the crowd, stay cool, make it about the match, show respect) and they find the words, written for what the other wrestler just said. Since v244 it's three decisions: the opening, one exchange call and the closer (a hot feud between two great talkers gets a second exchange call). How the exchange went decides the closer (mic drop when you're way ahead, a brawl, stare-down or match made when it's even, the other wrestler's last word when you're behind). Good talkers sometimes wind up a big line instead of answering, which you can cut off or let them finish. The extra calls are centred, so promos swing more without rating higher. The rival's promos play the same exchange.
- **🎬 Twists are occasional now (v233).** In a promo with a back-and-forth, your answer to the other wrestler usually closes the segment and counts as a full call. The old random twist (a run-in, the mic cutting out, a chant) only comes on top about a quarter of the time, so it's a surprise again. Crowd hijacks, the mystery figure and the countdown clock still come at their usual rate. Solo promos and turns keep their twist.
- **🔁 Back-and-forth promos (v228).** In a two-person Callout, Champion's Address, Contract Signing or Sit-Down Interview, the other wrestler fires back after your first call, and your next three options answer what they actually said. There are 230+ responses, picked by what you just did (insult, brag, promise, mind games, power play, challenge, handshake or respect, rough-up or a big attack) and whether it landed, the other wrestler's alignment and mic skill (weak talkers get short, blunt or tongue-tied lines; the sharpest ones need a great talker), titles on either side, feud heat, popularity gaps, a PPV coming up and the setting (no crowd lines in a studio). The response beat counts for a little less than a normal call, and the rival's promos get it too.
- **🎤 The mic drop.** When every call in a promo has landed, the twist can come with a fourth option: drop the mic for a guaranteed strong finish that ends the segment early, or push your luck for more.
- **👥 The crowd as a character.** On a finish (or a big moment in a main event, title match or PPV) where the crowd has a reason to lean — a hot feud, a big popularity gap, a title, a PPV — they sometimes make their wishes known: give them what they want for an extra ¼★, or go against them for heat. Rarely, in a live promo, they hijack it by chanting for someone on your roster who's more over than anyone in the ring: fight it, bring them out (a rub, a rivalry or a brand-new star) or cut to commercial. Never more than one crowd moment per show.
- **😤 Backstage confrontations.** Real backstage heat between two of your wrestlers can boil over between shows: separate them (a four-week truce — booking them together breaks it, and the Book screen warns you), let it simmer, or book them against each other that week, where the bad blood is guaranteed to come out in the match.
- **💬 Locker room whispers.** Now and then someone on your card pitches a specific spot for their match, based on how they wrestle, the stipulation and their alignment — tap it on the Book screen. Go for it and their match is guaranteed a call built around the idea; pass and they're a little let down. At most one a week.
- **🎧 Gorilla pitches.** Surprise run-ins and promo interruptions in your live segments used to just happen and start rivalries on their own. Now the wrestler pitches the idea to you in gorilla first — same moments, no more often. Send them out and the scene plays as before; turn it down and the segment goes on as booked. It's ±2 morale either way.

## Morale, pitches & grievances
- **🚪 The line outside your office (v234, restyled v235).** Wrestlers with ideas, demands and requests, and every other out-of-the-ring call that comes with its own dialogue — front-office situations, injury decisions, tampering rumors, trade offers, countdown and open-challenge signing offers, prospect tapes and emails, teams in the making — line up outside your office at the start of the week. Since v322 wrestlers also bring the requests the briefing used to only mention: an owed title shot, a contract clause (title shot, main events, PPVs), a win promised for doing the job, a stale feud that needs its ending. Say yes (it jumps to the top of Needs your attention; keep your word +4 morale, break it −8) or not this week (a little morale; they ask again in 3 weeks, or when a deadline hits). A contract title shot has a third answer since v323: promise it for the next PPV — the title match goes on that card (it follows the belt), the clause's deadline moves to meet it, and they stop asking. One visit per wrestler a week. Up to 3 a week, decided when the week starts. You see who's waiting and call them in one at a time ("2 of 3"); answer each to call in the next; since v324, agents of wrestlers on the last two shows of their contract, your scout's watchlist updates and ROH prospects ready for a call-up come to the door too, and clearing the line takes you back to work (the news is on Home's This week panel). Nothing goes live with one unanswered, and Home shows a strip if any are waiting.
- **A shorter line (v341).** Only 3 (4 since v370) get in each week, most urgent first — a front-office situation, a pitch, a contract on its last show and the trainer always get in — and everyone else waits for a later week. Agents come two shows out only for the names that matter (55+ popularity or a champion), everyone else on the last show; wrestler requests are capped at 2 a week and tapes arrive less often. The call-up, Not yet, watchlist and tape buttons in the office now move the line on (they used to leave the page sitting there).
- **Only real decisions knock (v342).** Things that were already owed and already tracked no longer come to the door: an earned title shot, a favor owed for doing the job, main-event and PPV clauses (all on the Book screen's checklist and tagged on the card, with the contract or promise enforcing them) and a feud going stale (a row in This week). A contract title shot still asks — tonight or the next PPV is a real choice — and once it's promised for a PPV they don't ask again. Scout watchlist updates and suggested tag teams are rows in This week too, with links to the ROH screen and the Front office, where those actions always are. ("Ready for the call-up" left the door in v342 and came back in v343.)
- **Morale moves every week.** TV time, wins, main events, kept promises and answered pitches lift it. Sitting at home wears on people (stars after 2 weeks off TV, everyone else after 3), clean losses sting (more for stars, most when they lose to someone less popular), and being worn down or underpaid grinds on them. Tap any wrestler to see their **Mood** and the reasons behind it. Mood shows in their work (v353): under 50 morale they phone it in (worse matches and promos, more the lower it goes), over 75 they go the extra mile — it's the **Morale** line in Why that rating?. **Keeping your word pays (v354):** a wrestler getting what they were owed — a pitch you said yes to, a title shot, main event or PPV spot in their contract, or a champion finally defending — works harder that night (**Getting what they were owed** in Why that rating?) and the payoff brings in fans, up to 3 a show. Break your word and the whole locker room hears (−1 morale to everyone else); a broken contract term also pays out 6 weeks of salary.
- **Backstage friends (v361).** After the draft your locker room is secretly split into friend groups, each around a top star. When something big happens to someone (a promise kept or broken, a pitch trial, a title, main-event or PPV win or loss), their friends feel about a third of it, so how you treat a midcarder can lift or sour a main eventer. Pairs are revealed through Mood ("Happy for…", "Upset about how… was treated") and show as Backstage friends on profiles; now and then a top star comes to the office about a friend. Dealt fresh every game; the offline rival keeps its simpler morale.
- **Locker-room mood and a louder chant (v362).** When the roster's average morale is under 66, every match and promo loses a little (shown as Locker room mood in Why that rating?; This week on Home flags it). It's a cost only, never a bonus. Booking the wrestler the crowd chants for now gives that segment +½★ and 5K fans (was +¼★, 2K). Together they make keeping your word pay: the attentive GM wins clearly more seasons than one who ignores the checklist.
- **Friends come out slowly, and drift (v363).** Until a friendship is known, Mood still gives the reason ("Happy for a friend", "Upset about how a friend was treated"), so morale never moves without one. Each shared moment (a promise kept or broken, a pitch trial, a new champion) can reveal the pair, and the third always does; the office visit does too. Now and then someone falls out with their group (likelier with real backstage heat), spends a few weeks alone, then joins another. Known pairs that split are announced.
- **Your building (v364).** TV nights happen in your own building: the Piddly-Ass Auditorium (start), the Good Building ($400K move-in, $40K/wk; tickets ×1.35, box office ×1.5, +10% fans) and the Actual Arena ($1M, $90K/wk; ×1.7, ×2, +20%). Crowd pull (last 3 shows' buzz plus tonight's show, plus or minus locker-room mood) has to fill it, or the seats are empty (tickets ×0.6, flat crowd −0.1★, −2K fans). Front office card to move up or down (2-week move-in, 8-week minimum stay), a fill verdict under Show flow on the Book screen, rent in Finances, and the building on the show poster. The rival moves up by its own rule. PPV stadium shows are planned.
- **Book your building each week (v424).** Buildings are unlocked once in the Front office (Good Building $400K, Actual Arena $1M after it) and then picked for each TV show on the Book screen, paying only the nights you use them: Good Building $55K a show (tickets ×1.45, box office ×1.6, +15% fans), Actual Arena $130K (×1.9, ×2.2, +30%). The Book screen shows each owned building with whether tonight's card fills it; your pick carries over, PPVs pay no building fee, and an unaffordable building drops you back to the Auditorium. Saves keep every building they had moved into (or had a lease lined up for), with no refunds. **Big audience, small room:** past 700K fans your top stars (85+) expect the Good Building and past 1.4M the Arena, whenever you can afford it; three TV shows in a row below that and they lose a point of morale each show, and the biggest comes to the office door — promise the bigger room next week (kept: +3 to the top of the card; broken: −3, and −6 for whoever asked) or say you're not there yet (−3 for them). The offline rival unlocks and books by its own rule.
- **Simpler office answers (v382).** Pitches have two answers: **Go for it** or **Sorry, not right now** (the trial option is gone; trials already running finish as normal). Friend visits are headed by how the wrestler looks ("Jade Cargill is looking wistful"), and the Show flow graph no longer explains rub matches.
- **Friends tell you when they split (v364).** A wrestler who falls out with their group comes to the office to tell you they're done with a friend: offer condolences if you knew (+2 morale), or ask "Wait, you were friends?" if you didn't.
- **Friends and the network get their turn (v369).** The network exec and friends' visits now come right after front-office business and ahead of contract talks that can still wait, and a friend's visit waits outside up to 4 weeks when the line is full (it used to give up after a week, so most splits were never told). A split that never gets its visit still makes the news.
- **A fourth seat outside the office (v370).** The line now lets 4 people in each week instead of 3, so the network exec and friends' visits almost always make it the week they arrive.
- **Nothing falls through (v371).** The network exec and friends' visits now always get in, like a pitch or a front-office situation, so they never wait behind the 4-person limit. Splits queue up one a week instead of replacing each other, injured wrestlers still come by to talk, and if a visit can't happen (the wrestler left), the news still tells you.
- **😤 Prove them wrong (v365).** Wrestlers left undrafted who sign with a human GM in season 1 arrive with a chip on their shoulder: +10 morale, +1 raw in their matches and promos for 4 weeks (two at most per segment, its own line in Why that rating?) and +1 popularity for each of those weeks on TV. Same price as anyone; it makes a lighter draft that fills out in free agency its own path. draft-sweep (FA path, 24 x 2): 85% of the cap wins 71/42% of seasons vs to-the-cap 58/71% (was 50/33 vs 75/50).
- **🌟 Star egos (v366).** A star (80+ popularity) who isn't a champion and hasn't been featured for 2 weeks loses morale every week, scaled by how many stars you carry: about 1 a week with 10 stars on your roster, 0.5 with 5, 1.5 with 15 (past 10 it reads "Lost in a crowded locker room"). Stacking stars now has a cost you manage by rotating the spotlight. draft-sweep (44 x 2, FA path, seasons won): to the cap 61/61% vs 85% of the cap 61/48% (was 73/64 vs 55/50).
- **Pitches about every two weeks** (more often when the locker room is unhappy): main events, title shots, mic time, feuds, partners, rest, raises — plus **a spot on the next PPV** and **a dream match** with a bigger star.
- **Grievances escalate** when morale sinks, as front-office decisions you make before the next show: a **pay demand** (under 55), a **walkout threat** (under 45), a **shoot promo** on live TV or grievances at the **media scrum** (under 35), and an actual **walkout** for a few weeks (under 25, or after you call their bluff). Below 10, they may still demand their release.

## Tampering & injuries
- **Tiredness bites (v424).** Matches drain more stamina (singles 18, was 16; PPV +6, was +4) and a wrestler who works recovers only 7 a week (was 10; a week off still gives 22, was 25). A star who wrestles every week runs down in about five weeks, so plan their weeks off and keep them on TV with promos. In a sweep, an attentive GM's roster averages about 87 stamina (was 93) with 6–8 injuries a season (was ~5); a GM who never rests anyone gets 12–14.
- **🕵️ Tampering.** When an unhappy or underpaid wrestler is within 8 weeks of the end of their deal, the rival office (in a league, "another company") may call them. The line outside your office brings the rumor with the offer: match it and extend, negotiate, sit down with them, or let them go. Ignore it and, when the deal runs out, they sign with the rival on the spot (in a league they hit free agency). Re-sign them any way (the rumor's buttons, Negotiate, or the roster) and the rumor clears from your office line right away.
- **🚑 Injury decisions.** When someone on your roster is hurt for a few weeks, choose: let it heal; rush them back in about half the time (fragile for 6 weeks after, with much higher re-injury risk); or, for longer injuries, surgery — more time out and a fee, but they come back tougher (+5 durability). The trainer brings the decision to your office door.

## The history book
Open it from the Titles screen or the Office:
- **Title lineages** — every reign of every title, with weeks held, defenses and how it ended.
- **Records** — most wins, title reigns, five-star matches, main events and PPV wins, the biggest legacies and the greatest matches.
- **Awards** — the year-end awards, season by season.
- **Hall of Fame** — at each year-end, retiring wrestlers with a big enough legacy are inducted automatically, and every GM gets one pick of their own for the class.
- Tap any wrestler to see their **career line** and **head-to-head** records against the people they've faced most.

History is recorded from v157 on (existing champions are picked up the first time you open a save).

## Trades
- Propose from the **Office** or the **🔁 Trade** button on another brand's wrestler: up to two wrestlers each way, plus cash either way. Champions can't be traded, and nobody can go over 30 wrestlers.
- **Solo:** the rival answers on the spot — accept, counter (they want more cash) or decline — based on each wrestler's overall, upside and contract length. On Hard it drives a harder bargain. Every few weeks the rival may call with an offer of its own; it waits outside your office for a week.
- **Leagues:** offers wait outside the other GM's office until they accept or decline (or you withdraw). Offers expire after 3 weeks.
- Traded wrestlers keep their contracts. Happy ones take it a little hard; unhappy ones welcome the fresh start.

## The road to the PPV
- Every PPV has a **hype meter** (Home and the Book screen) that builds over the weeks before it: good TV shows add hype and flat ones cost some, hot feuds you keep going add a little, and **announced PPV matches** (Contract Signings, or callouts booked for the PPV) add a lot — up to four.
- **🎟️ The title picture** (top of the Titles screen) lists every title shot you owe — Rumble, tournament and Casino Gauntlet winners, title-shot pitches you agree to ("win two and you're next" makes them the #1 contender), contract clauses — with the date and the feud heat against the champion. Earning a shot starts the rivalry (🔥15). Build it past the gold line (🔥35) before the night: inside 4 weeks the Book screen checklist reminds you (and the challenger may knock on your office door) and **🎤 Build it** books a face-to-face promo. If the belt changes hands first, the shot follows it. The Home screen's Titles tile shows how many shots you owe and how many are behind.
- The **go-home show** right before the PPV matters most: a strong one adds a lot, a flat one costs you.
- On the night, hype moves **ticket sales** (−15% to +15%) and the crowd: a hot build brings extra fans, a cold one costs some. Then the meter resets.

## Party night (same room, big screen)
Party night is a way to play an online league, not a separate game. It uses the same league, the same save and the same code, so you can play some weeks together on the couch and the rest on your own time.

- **Start it:** any GM taps **Start party night** (Home, or the ⚙️ Game menu). Everyone in the room books **at the same time** — creating wrestlers too — and then goes live one at a time on the TV. The draft still goes pick by pick.
- **Lock in, then go live on the TV:** book your card and tap **Lock in my card**. You can unlock it until everyone else is in. Once every card is locked, GMs go live **one at a time on the TV**. Your phone says "You're live!" and you play your show there. Any highlight reel (promo beats, match calls, choices and outcomes) is shown on the TV so the room can watch and shout advice; the reveal shows how the rest of your card went. When the last GM wraps up, the week airs and every phone gets its results.
- **Picks on the TV:** when the live GM taps a promo or match choice, the TV lights up that option for a moment, then shows how it played out with a "Picked" tag on top. Your phone does the same on every show, party night or not: the call you tap lights up with a ✓ Picked tag while the others dim, and the outcome keeps a "You picked" strip on top.
- **The TV:** on any screen, open the game, tap **Show a league on this screen** and enter the league code. The TV follows the league live. It shows who's still booking, the draft board (with each pick's Ring / Mic / Pop), the standings and whoever is live right now (choices and all). Then it plays the week's results — a tug of war between two shows, or a Battle Royale with three or four — segment by segment, before the winner is announced. It doesn't take a seat, and it can **Replay last week**. It fills any size screen: a PC running a 4K TV at 100% Windows scaling gets the same layout as 1080p, just sharper (150% and 200% scaling look the same too). If the league still has open seats, the TV shows a QR code so friends can join.
- **The locker-room feed:** while everyone books, the TV plays each office visit the moment a GM answers it. The ring bell rings, the wrestler's own line comes up, and the GM's answer is stamped underneath ("✓ Maria says yes", "✕ Josh says no"). That GM's tile shows **🚪 In the office** while theirs is up, and answered visits fold into a short list underneath. Visits that are strategy (rival tampering, the trainer, trade offers and prospect tapes) are only teased: the room sees who got the call, never the money, the weeks out or the trade.
- **Sound on the TV:** on party night the TV plays the sounds for the room and the phones stay quiet. Tap **🔊** in the TV header to switch them off and on (after a reload, the TV needs one tap anywhere before it can make sound).
- **Everyone at once:** phones save at the same time, and each save is merged into the league. If two GMs grab the same thing in the same moment (say, the same free agent), the first one gets it and the other phone refreshes — keeping that GM's card, lock-in and private plans. Two GMs forming different tag teams or factions at once both stick.
- **End it:** **End party night** in the ⚙️ Game menu (or the link in the banner). The league carries on as a normal league from exactly where it is — everyone books on their own phone whenever suits them. GMs who already went live that week stay locked in. If someone has to leave early, end party night and they book their show later with turn alerts as usual.
- **Solo TV (v262):** try the TV on your own. In a solo game, **⚙️ Game menu → Solo TV → Put my game on a TV** gives you a code; enter it on the TV with **Show a league on this screen**. The TV treats it as a one-GM party night: your booking night and office answers, your live show mirrored as you make the calls, then the results reveal, with the sound on the TV and your phone quiet. The computer GM counts as locked in. Use a different device or browser for the TV.
- **Staying in sync (v262):** phones and the TV reconnect on their own if the live connection drops, check the league with the server every 15 seconds on party night (and whenever the app comes back to the front), and catch up on any update that arrived while a save was going through — no refreshing to see whose turn it is. A save that hasn't gone through yet is never overwritten by the next update; it's merged in instead. The TV feed (the live GM's screen and office answers) moved to its own small document so it no longer makes every phone re-download the whole league several times a minute — that needs the latest `firebase/firestore.rules` published (until then it falls back to the old way).
- **The overnights on the TV:** when two shows go head to head, the TV plays the results as the same quarter-hour chart as the solo reveal — it runs itself segment by segment, a couple of seconds apart, with a beat before the main event. Three or four shows still get the Battle Royale. The TV also updates itself to new versions between segments, so it never runs older code than the phones.
- Needs the Firebase setup in [ONLINE-SETUP.md](ONLINE-SETUP.md) and an internet connection on every device. No new setup beyond online leagues.

The tool (about 15MB) downloads when it runs and isn't stored on the device, so cutting out needs a connection; if it can't run, the photo is kept as it was. A batch saves each photo as it goes and stops cleanly if the device runs out of room. **Clean up the edges** re-does cut-outs made before v172 in place, peeling off the light outline around heads and hair. ## Wrestler voices and match calls
The 58 AEW and WWE wrestlers who start with at least 80 popularity have original dialogue for challenges, acceptances, replies, and solo callouts. Signature moves can appear in pinfall calls, and some stars also have named near-fall moves or submission holds. Established pairings such as the Young Bucks and the Usos can call their own double-team finish. Choose the extra near fall and the match keeps going even if the escape looks awkward.

## After Dark
An optional mode under **⚙️ Game menu → After Dark** (off by default). Any two wrestlers in an eligible live promo face-off or singles highlight can get up to two extra beats. Your selected tone applies to the segment, including on party-night TV.
- **Grudge** adds vicious threats, commentary keyed to the rough spots you called, and more heated aftermath when you book a hostile closing shot.
- **Tension** adds suggestive back-and-forth and charged pauses. A respectful handshake can linger.
- **Auto** uses Grudge once a feud is heated and Tension otherwise. Choose a tone directly to use it across eligible pairings. Extra beats leave match results and ratings alone.

## Your games

## Your saves
Tap **Sign in with Google** in **⚙️ Game menu → Saves & backups** to back up automatically and continue on any device — sign in on the other device and all your games show up (v383). No Google account? **Use a sync code instead** still works the old way (needs the one-time Firebase setup in [ONLINE-SETUP.md](ONLINE-SETUP.md)). The game also keeps automatic backups of your last few weeks under **⚙️ Game menu → Saves & backups → Backups on this device**.

- **Every solo game syncs both ways.** Each game has its own slot under your sync code. Enter the code on another device and all your games appear in ⇄ Your games there; play any of them anywhere and the others catch up.
- **Saving stays safe.** Everything saves on the device first and the cloud catches up in the background (offline play queues). An upload only lands if nobody else saved that game since this device last synced it, so nothing is overwritten blindly. If both devices played the same game before syncing, you're asked which save to keep — never decided for you — and the other is kept as a backup. A newer cloud save never replaces unsynced play on this device, the game you're playing gets a backup first, and nothing swaps while you're mid-show or mid-edit.
- **Deleting is reversible.** Deleting a game removes it from your other devices, but its cloud copy stays: **Backups → Deleted games** brings it back everywhere. A device that played a game after it was deleted elsewhere keeps it and puts it back.
- **Pick up and play (v409).** With cloud save on, the game you're playing now follows you between your computer and your phone: put one down, pick up the other, and you carry on from the same week. The computer checks for your phone's progress whenever its window comes back into focus (and every so often while it sits open), sends your changes up the moment you switch away, and stays on the screen you were on instead of jumping back to Home. TV mode, solo TV and live shows are never touched.
- **Backups don't crowd out your games (v408).** The automatic backups (each game's last 5 weeks, plus up to 3 snapshots before anything replaces it) now live in the phone's big app storage instead of the small ~5MB space the game you're playing uses, so switching between saved games no longer fails with "your device may be out of storage". Identical copies of the same game are cleared out automatically, and backups from older versions move over the first time you open the game.
- **Updating from v195 or earlier** is automatic: the game cloud save used to follow keeps one shared id on every device, games that already had a cloud copy are matched up instead of uploaded twice, and a device still on an older version keeps syncing until it updates. No Firebase rule changes are needed.

Saves and photos live on each device, inside the installed app. The installed app does **not** share saves with a copy opened from Files or a browser tab, so use **⚙️ Game menu → Export this game** in the old copy and **Import** in the new one to move a season over (and **Export Photo Pack / Import Photo Pack** for pictures). Exporting now and then is a good backup.
