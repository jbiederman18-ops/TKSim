# Tony Khan Simulator

A wrestling general-manager game: draft from AEW and WWE's rosters, book Dynamite and Collision through a full year — a PPV every 4 weeks, alternating AEW and WWE's biggest shows from the Royal Rumble through All In to Survivor Series — and win the fan war. Works fully offline once installed.

## Put it on GitHub Pages
1. Create a new public repository on GitHub (e.g. `all-elite-gm`).
2. Upload every file in this folder to the repository root (**Add file → Upload files**).
3. Go to **Settings → Pages**, set **Source** to *Deploy from a branch*, choose `main` and `/ (root)`, and save.
4. After a minute your game is live at `https://<your-username>.github.io/all-elite-gm/`.

## Install on your phone
- **iPhone (Safari):** open the link, tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**.
- **Android (Chrome):** open the link, tap **⋮ → Install app** (or *Add to Home screen*).

Open it once while online; after that it launches with no connection.

## Updating the game
Upload the changed files (usually `index.html`, plus any `.js` files that changed, such as `season.js` or `tutorial.js`), then open `sw.js` and bump the version in `VERSION` (e.g. `aegm-v36` → `aegm-v37`) so phones pick up the update. Installed copies update the next time they're opened online (close and reopen the app once).

## Play online with a friend
Host a league for 2–4 GMs and friends join with a 6-letter code. Everyone runs their own brand, you face a different GM each week, and the most fans gained by Survivor Series wins. Everyone creates wrestlers and books each week at the same time (the week airs when the last GM locks in); the draft goes in order, and you can queue up to 10 picks so they're made for you the moment you're on the clock. Offline changes sync later, and each player gets a notification when it's their move. One-time setup: see [ONLINE-SETUP.md](ONLINE-SETUP.md).

## New players: the tour
A new game offers a one-minute **draft tour**, then a short **first-week tour** once the draft is done: your show and how you win, Needs your attention, Suggest the rest, booking by feel, feuds and going live. Everything else is a one-time **coach tip** that pops up the first time it matters — the match editor, your first results, PPV week, the AEW/WWE calendar, the running order, show flow, stamina, the Roster, Free agents, Titles and Office screens — at most one booking tip a week so they never pile up. Replay it any time from **⚙️ → Rookie orientation** (where you can also turn coach tips on or off).

The tour reads its numbers straight from the game and is checked automatically on every update (`tools/check-tutorial.py`), so it keeps up as the rules change. When a stop changes, players who already took the tour get a short **What's new** replay of just those stops.

## AEW meets WWE
- **One talent pool.** WWE's Raw and SmackDown rosters (current to Money in the Bank, Oct 10, 2026) are in the draft and free agency alongside AEW's. Ratings reflect each wrestler's current ability and popularity on the same scale — WWE's biggest names are the most popular wrestlers in the game. Real WWE tag teams and factions come together (The Bloodline, The Vision, Judgment Day, LWO, The Lucha Bros…), and anyone currently hurt or on leave starts out injured in an existing save.
- **Supplemental draft for older saves.** A save that started before the WWE update can hold a one-time WWE Supplemental Draft from the Home screen: 3 rounds, only the unsigned WWE wrestlers, the brand behind in the fan war picks first, and a budget on top of each brand's payroll with room for two big stars and a solid third pick. It runs on the normal draft screen and drops back into the same week; anyone not picked stays a free agent. You can skip it instead.
- **Photos.** 🔍 Search for a WWE wrestler looks on wwe.com for their official photo; in **Quick photo mode**, tap **WWE** to run through just the WWE roster.
- **A bigger draft budget.** The draft salary cap is 20% higher than before (about $527K/wk in a two-GM game), so each brand can afford more top stars for star-vs-star matches. Revenue grows only half as much, so the extra payroll is a real cost and the bank stays tighter.
- **WWE's world titles** — Undisputed WWE, World Heavyweight, WWE Women's and WWE Women's World — join the ten AEW belts, so each show starts with seven.
- **Two supershows.** WrestleMania (week 12) and All In (week 24) are the climaxes of the WWE and AEW halves of the year: hotter crowds, double the fan swing, bigger gates and bigger venue bills.
- **The calendar** alternates the biggest shows: Royal Rumble, Revolution, WrestleMania, Double or Nothing, Money in the Bank, All In, SummerSlam, All Out, Full Gear and Survivor Series, which closes the year. The Owen Hart Cups now end at Double or Nothing and the Continental Classic at Full Gear.
- **🏟️ Royal Rumble.** A men's (8) and a women's (6) Rumble lock onto your Royal Rumble card, which runs five matches in all (Money in the Bank runs six). You pick the entrants, the winner and who's eliminated last. The winner earns a world title shot at WrestleMania against your best champion in their division (belts never leave their show, so if yours are vacant the winner is first in line when one is crowned).
- **💼 Money in the Bank.** A men's (6) and a women's (5) ladder match lock onto your Money in the Bank card. The winner holds the briefcase for a year and can cash in on a world champion of their division on your brand at any time: book them against the champion and make it the cash-in, or open any match the champion is in and have the holder cash in straight after it. You decide whether the cash-in works; it lifts the match it follows (+½★ when it succeeds). Unused briefcases expire at the next Money in the Bank, and the rival cashes in too.
- **Survivor Series is the crossover show** (it took Forbidden Door's place): up to two dream matches against the rival's stars, and rival invasions in the TV weeks before it.
- **Existing saves** keep their week: the WWE wrestlers arrive in free agency, WWE's titles start vacant (two for each brand), and the PPVs take their new names from here on.

Tap it, or **WK** in the header, for the full calendar: each PPV and what's special about it, TV specials, tournament rounds, crossover season, promised matches and the week each of your contracts runs out.

## The countdown

Now and then a countdown clock appears during a solo promo. Three weeks later a free agent from the other side of the face/heel line debuts and goes after the wrestler who had the mic (instant heat), and you get one week to sign them at a discount — bigger if the promo hyped the clock. Sign them early, or let the rival, and they debut for whoever did.

## Scouting & ROH (the farm system)
- **Three scouting boards** (Roster → Free agents → Scouting), refreshing every few weeks: **🌱 Indies** (raw prospects and gimmick acts, the most upside), **🏟️ ROH** (polished regulars plus the real ROH roster — Jay Lethal, Matt Sydal, Top Flight, the Von Erichs, Premier Athletes, Dalton Castle, Lacey Lane and more. They aren't in the AEW draft, so ROH is the only place to get them. Ready sooner, less upside) and **🌏 Overseas** (skilled imports from Japan, Mexico and the UK who are still learning promos).
- **Known names from the PWI 500.** Every Indies and Overseas board also carries one or two real names from outside AEW and WWE — men ranked on the 2026 PWI 500 (Leon Slater, Nic Nemeth, Mustafa Ali, Michael Oku, Yota Tsuji, Zack Sabre Jr., Templario, Atlantis Jr. and more, with their rank on the card) plus top independent and joshi women (Masha Slamovich, Sareee, Saya Kamitani, Starlight Kid, Lady Flammer…). They cost more than prospects but arrive closer to ready.
- **ROH books itself every week.** Everyone on your ROH roster works a match on HonorClub against ROH regulars or other GMs' prospects, builds an ROH record and grows toward their potential. ROH's four titles (World, Women's World, TV, Pure) are defended every other week by the best records. Wins, good matches and ROH gold build **call-up buzz** (🧊 → 🔥), which fades if they cool off. When someone is hot, the briefing says it's time to bring them up — and the hotter they are, the bigger their AEW debut. The ROH screen shows the champions, last week's results, the standings and your watchlist.
- **Tapes & emails.** Most weeks a prospect sends you their tape or an email in the briefing. Pass, **keep following** them (up to 6 — they send updates as they improve, get a little pricier, and may sign elsewhere), or sign them straight to ROH.
- **Sign to AEW or to ROH.** AEW signings take a roster spot and can be booked right away. ROH signings go to your ROH roster (Roster → 🏟️ ROH, up to 8): no AEW roster spot, half salary, and they grow toward their potential every week on ROH TV, slower than the Performance Center.
- **Feature** one ROH wrestler to grow faster and build popularity (up to 45). Send someone on a once-per-career **10-week excursion**: NJPW (biggest in-ring jump and a tougher body), CMLL (comes back a bigger draw) or RevPro (real mic reps). They return with more potential and a bigger debut pop.
- **Call up** anyone whenever you need them (they get a debut pop), and **send down** rookies or anyone at 45 popularity or lower to develop. Champions can't be sent down. ROH deals run out like any contract — extend them from the ROH screen.
- **Teams reunite.** Sign or call up both halves of a real ROH team (Top Flight, the Von Erichs, Premier Athletes…) and they're a team again on your roster.
- Works in online leagues too: every GM has their own ROH roster.

## Offers & contracts
- **Free agents with 55+ popularity want an offer**, not just a fee: a signing bonus, a weekly salary, and promises — 🎬 creative control, ⭐ main event spots, 🏆 a title shot, 📺 a place on every PPV. Each wrestler wants different things (a big star wants control, a hungry midcarder wants main events), and the offer screen tells you how it looks to them. Smaller names still sign on the spot.
- **Sealed bids.** Solo, the rival may send an offer too and the wrestler picks right away. In a league, every other GM can send one sealed counter before the week airs, and the wrestler picks the best package when it does. On party night the TV reveals every offer in a "Signing war" before the results.
- **Contract countdown.** Deals with 5 weeks or less left (9 for stars) show on a ✍️ card on Home and at the top of the weekly briefing, amber at 3 weeks and red on the last show. The Book screen repeats it, and going live on someone's last show asks you to re-sign them or let them go first
- **Re-signing is a negotiation** (from the weekly briefing or the wrestler's card). They make demands; agree to some or all, or counter on money. They take it, counter back (up to three rounds) or walk away, depending on their morale, how hot your show is and whether you've kept your word before.
- **Promises are contract terms.** The Book screen shows what's owed. Keep one and morale rises (and a little lost trust comes back); break one and morale drops; after three, a wrestler may walk out. An injury pauses the clock on a promise. Only true main eventers ask for creative control, it lasts 12 weeks, and a roster can have at most 3 people with it. **Creative control** means they won't agree to lose. Booked to lose, they show on the checklist with **Talk to them** (and again at go-live): offer to protect them in the finish (a cheat, an upset roll-up or a count-out instead of a clean pin), a big win down the line (a main-event or PPV win within 4 weeks), or their win back (beat the same opponent by the next PPV). Each is easier to sell than the last; morale, broken promises, how hot your show is and who they're losing to all count. Go live without a deal and about half the time they change the finish and go over (−¼★); otherwise they do the job under protest and morale drops hard. A promised win stays on the checklist until it happens — miss the deadline and it's a broken promise.

## Booking by feel
- **Match vs wrestler, at a glance.** Anything about the match (Talent, Chem, Story, Extras — in the buzz bar, Match details and on booked slots) is a gold segmented meter; a wrestler's own ratings (Ring, Star) are silver dots in a box hanging off their card, topped in their face/heel color, with how they match up (Chem, Feud) under a "VS …" divider.
- **The crowd buzz bar.** Once both sides are picked, the match editor shows the crowd buzz and the four ingredient pips (Talent, Chem, Story, Extras) in a bar that pins to the top as you scroll — change the stipulation, finish or run-in and watch it move. The full Match details, with the reason behind each row, stay at the bottom.
- **✨ Suggest the rest is the floor, not the ceiling.** It covers the must-dos (promised and contracted matches, contract clauses, promises from pitches, the most overdue title defense, the weekly goal, the crowd's chant) and fills the rest with a plain card — standard matches, clean finishes, no run-ins, no PPV announcements, empty bonus slots. In the balance sweep a Suggest-only GM on Normal wins about 30% of nights and about half its seasons; a hands-on booker (`--engaged`) wins clearly more.
- **Match details.** Every match shows its ingredients — Talent, Chemistry, Story and Extras, up to five pips each with the reason beside it — plus anything holding it back. Promos show Talent, Fit, Story and your live calls. Under each wrestler in the editor: ring (or mic) and star pips, morale, stamina and anything they've asked for. Tap **?** by Chemistry for the style chart. The wrestler picker can sort by 🏆 Champions and 🎤 Requests.
- **Needs your attention.** The Book screen's checklist of everything that matters for this show, checked live against the card. Anything still blocking a clean go-live sits on top, the rest under Everything else: Before you go live (slots still to book, requests, promised matches, promises, contracts ending tonight — going live with one open asks you first), Chances tonight, Keep in mind (including one row for every title that needs defending, led by the most overdue) and Front office (including a warning when your roster runs thin). Most items have a button straight to the fix.
- **The briefing is a rundown.** A count up top, then *Before you go live*, *Chances tonight*, *Backstage* and the *Front office*. Each place backstage is one card with one voice: unhappy wrestlers are chips in *The hallway* (with their mood), tired ones in *The trainer's room*, overdue titles are rows with their own Defend button, and every contract coming up is one *On the phone* list, soonest first, with what's at stake (a title, the briefcase, an unpaid feud). Advice shared by a whole card is said once, and anything new since last week is tagged NEW.
- **Repeats are like for like.** A singles rematch only counts as a repeat of last week's singles match, and a multi-person match only of last week's multi-person one, so a Rumble doesn't spoil next week's one-on-one.
- **No star math while booking.** The match editor shows crowd buzz (🥶 → 🔥🔥🔥) and ▲/▼ tells on titles and finishes instead of exact star bonuses. Stipulations show a rough upside (📈 big / some / little, or 📉 works against them) weighed against the extra wear (🔋), injury risk (🩹) and anyone it would leave gassed. Risky calls (first-time pairings, gimmick matches, screwy finishes, run-ins, rookies) make a match a 🎲 wild card.
- **Rumbles and gauntlets don't settle feuds.** Rivals who meet in a Royal Rumble, Money in the Bank or the Casino Gauntlet build heat, but the feud still needs its own match to be blown off — even on a PPV.
- **💥 TV blow-offs.** A feud at 35+ heat doesn't have to wait for a PPV: in the match editor, tap **Make it the blow-off**. A clean win (or an upset) ends it on weekly TV, and a gimmick stipulation makes it a fitting end with a bigger morale and popularity boost. A dirty finish or no contest leaves it unsettled. It's always a smaller payoff than a PPV blow-off.
- **Pick who takes the pin.** In any match with three or more wrestlers, only one person on the losing end takes the fall and everyone else stays protected. The match editor lets you choose who (the default is the lowest-billed), and shows how much of a rub the winner gets for pinning them. A wrestler with creative control only objects if they're the one taking the pin.
- **Titles are split evenly.** A new game deals the titles out between the two shows (each gets seven, with at least one men's and one women's world title on each side — AEW's and WWE's world titles are spread across both). Each brand crowns its own vacant titles, and every belt stays on its show for good — there are no cross-show title challenges, so a belt can't be stolen. A champion who leaves the show (traded, poached by tampering, released) vacates the title, and Rumble, Money in the Bank, Casino Gauntlet and Owen Hart Cup shots are always against your own champions.
- **Reveal after the show.** Live promos end on the crowd's reaction (🥶 → 🔥🔥🔥) rather than a star rating, so every number waits for the results. In solo play the results come out like a broadcast, on one screen that never scrolls, as head-to-head rounds: your segment and the rival's in the same slot of the card share the spotlight, each one's stars sweep in smoothly (part-stars fill part of a star), and the better one gets ▲ Wins the round. Main events always meet in the final round. A rundown of both cards underneath fills in as you go, and the scoreboard keeps a running "so far" for both shows. The final ratings and the winner land last, then **Full results** opens the whole breakdown. Tap to hurry it along or **Skip ▸▸**; **Last Show** goes straight to the full results, with a **▶ Replay the reveal** button. Then tap **Why that rating?** under any of your matches to see what each part was worth, including the luck of the night.
- **Every promo has a job.** Callout (can get a match made), Sneak Attack (most heat, counts as physical contact), Contract Signing (books the PPV match), Sit-Down Interview (biggest morale lift and fastest mic growth — the fix for an unhappy wrestler), Faction Rally (the bigger star, listed first, rubs off on a less popular partner; alone, it lifts the whole group's spirits), Backstage Vignette (low risk, and grows wrestlers at popularity 60 or below faster), Champion's Address (raises the title's prestige when it lands) and Heel / Face Turn. The promo picker marks ✓ Good fit when one suits who you've picked, and Suggest the rest and the rival use the whole set.
- **Discovered chemistry.** Wrestlers who've never shared a ring only show chemistry "on paper" with a ❓; their first match rolls a spark that sticks as history.
- **A bonus match.** Weekly TV has an optional bonus match alongside the bonus promo. Book it when you have something worth showing or leave it empty — it counts toward the show rating like any segment and costs stamina like any match. ✨ Suggest the rest leaves it for you to decide.
- **💪 Squash matches.** Pick **Squash** in the match editor for a quick, one-sided win. It never rates highly (about 2½★ at most), so it's momentum at the cost of show quality — the bonus match is a natural home for it. Tap **Use local enhancement talent** to feed your wrestler an unsigned nobody from free agency (it costs nothing), or pick someone from your roster: the winner gets more out of beating a name, but the loser loses popularity and morale, and the more popular they are, the more it costs (a burial). The same wrestler squashing again within six weeks gets 60%, then 30%, then nothing, and squashing someone more popular than the winner backfires.
- **Stipulation specialties.** In the match editor, stipulations that suit someone's style are marked ⭐ with their name (e.g. ⭐ Specialty · Ospreay on a Ladder match), listed first, and the closed picker tells you how many there are. Ones that work against a style get a ⚠️. A booked match on a specialty stipulation shows the ⭐ on the Book screen.
- **Money stays meaningful.** A bigger company pays bigger wages when wrestlers sign or re-sign (about +15% at 1M fans), production and stipulations cost more in bigger venues, and past a big night's box office the extra comes in at half — so a hit promotion's bank levels off instead of piling up millions. The offline rival doesn't pay the wage premium (it runs a bigger roster); in online leagues every GM does. Selling out scales the same way: sponsor money grows with the company and so does the fan cost, so it's about the same trade at any size.
- **The top is hard to reach and hard to hold.** Popularity gains slow sharply past 80 and again past 90, and stars at 85+ slip a little in weeks they aren't featured (main event, PPV win, or a 3½★+ match or promo).
- **Multi-person matches** are carried by their top two names and their hottest rivalry, reward a mix of styles (triple threats and 4-ways love flyers and showmen; tags lean on partners who click), and pay off later: only one loser takes the fall, lower names gain popularity from the top star, and the top star uses less stamina.

## Morale, pitches & grievances
- **Morale moves every week.** TV time, wins, main events, kept promises and answered pitches lift it. Sitting at home wears on people (stars after 2 weeks off TV, everyone else after 3), clean losses sting (more for stars, most when they lose to someone less popular), and being worn down or underpaid grinds on them. Tap any wrestler to see their **Mood** and the reasons behind it.
- **Pitches about every two weeks** (more often when the locker room is unhappy): main events, title shots, mic time, feuds, partners, rest, raises — plus **a spot on the next PPV** and **a dream match** with a bigger star.
- **Grievances escalate** when morale sinks, as front-office decisions you make before the next show: a **pay demand** (under 55), a **walkout threat** (under 45), a **shoot promo** on live TV or grievances at the **media scrum** (under 35), and an actual **walkout** for a few weeks (under 25, or after you call their bluff). Below 10, they may still demand their release.

## Tampering & injuries
- **🕵️ Tampering.** When an unhappy or underpaid wrestler is within 8 weeks of the end of their deal, the rival office (in a league, "another company") may call them. The briefing flags the rumor with the offer: match it and extend, negotiate, sit down with them, or let them go. Ignore it and, when the deal runs out, they sign with the rival on the spot (in a league they hit free agency).
- **🚑 Injury decisions.** When someone on your roster is hurt for a few weeks, choose: let it heal; rush them back in about half the time (fragile for 6 weeks after, with much higher re-injury risk); or, for longer injuries, surgery — more time out and a fee, but they come back tougher (+5 durability). Decisions wait in the briefing, with a card on Home.

## The history book
Open it from the Titles screen or the Office:
- **Title lineages** — every reign of every title, with weeks held, defenses and how it ended.
- **Records** — most wins, title reigns, five-star matches, main events and PPV wins, the biggest legacies and the greatest matches.
- **Awards** — the year-end awards, season by season.
- **Hall of Fame** — at each year-end, retiring wrestlers with a big enough legacy are inducted automatically, and every GM gets one pick of their own for the class.
- Tap any wrestler to see their **career line** and **head-to-head** records against the people they've faced most.

History is recorded from v157 on (existing champions are picked up the first time you open a save).

## Trades
- Propose from the **Office** or the **🔁 Trade** button on another brand's wrestler: up to two wrestlers each way, plus cash either way. Champions can't be traded, and nobody can go over 30 wrestlers.
- **Solo:** the rival answers on the spot — accept, counter (they want more cash) or decline — based on each wrestler's overall, upside and contract length. On Hard it drives a harder bargain. Every few weeks the rival may call with an offer of its own; it waits in your briefing for a week.
- **Leagues:** offers wait in the other GM's briefing until they accept or decline (or you withdraw). Offers expire after 3 weeks.
- Traded wrestlers keep their contracts. Happy ones take it a little hard; unhappy ones welcome the fresh start.

## The road to the PPV
- Every PPV has a **hype meter** (Home and the Book screen) that builds over the weeks before it: good TV shows add hype and flat ones cost some, hot feuds you keep going add a little, and **announced PPV matches** (Contract Signings, or callouts booked for the PPV) add a lot — up to four.
- **🎟️ The title picture** (top of the Titles screen) lists every title shot you owe — Rumble, tournament and Casino Gauntlet winners, title-shot pitches you agree to ("win two and you're next" makes them the #1 contender), contract clauses — with the date and the feud heat against the champion. Earning a shot starts the rivalry (🔥15). Build it past the gold line (🔥35) before the night: inside 4 weeks the Book screen checklist and the weekly briefing remind you, **🎤 Build it** books a face-to-face promo, and Suggest the rest gives them a promo slot. If the belt changes hands first, the shot follows it. The Home screen's Titles tile shows how many shots you owe and how many are behind.
- The **go-home show** right before the PPV matters most: a strong one adds a lot, a flat one costs you.
- On the night, hype moves **ticket sales** (−15% to +15%) and the crowd: a hot build brings extra fans, a cold one costs some. Then the meter resets.

## Party night (same room, big screen)
Party night is a way to play an online league, not a separate game. It uses the same league, the same save and the same code, so you can play some weeks together on the couch and the rest on your own time.

- **Start it:** any GM opens the league's **Office → Party night → Start party night**. Everyone in the league now books **at the same time** instead of taking turns. That covers creating wrestlers too. The draft still goes pick by pick.
- **Lock in, then go live on the TV:** book your card and tap **Lock in my card**. You can unlock it until everyone else is in. Once every card is locked, GMs go live **one at a time on the TV**. Your phone says "You're live!" and you play your show there, but every promo beat, match call, choice and outcome is shown on the TV so the room can watch and shout advice. When the last GM wraps up, the week airs and every phone gets its results.
- **Picks on the TV:** when the live GM taps a promo or match choice, the TV lights up that option for a moment, then shows how it played out with a "Picked" tag on top. Your phone does the same on every show, party night or not: the call you tap lights up with a ✓ Picked tag while the others dim, and the outcome keeps a "You picked" strip on top.
- **The TV:** on any screen, open the game, tap **Show a league on this screen** and enter the league code. The TV follows the league live. It shows who's still booking, the draft board, the standings and whoever is live right now (choices and all). Then it plays each week's shows side by side, segment by segment, alternating between the two shows: stars sweep in, the better segment in each slot gets ▲ Wins the round, and the final ratings fill in before the winner is announced. It doesn't take a seat, and it can **Replay last week**. It fills any size screen: a PC running a 4K TV at 100% Windows scaling gets the same layout as 1080p, just sharper (150% and 200% scaling look the same too). If the league still has open seats, the TV shows a QR code so friends can join.
- **Everyone at once:** phones save at the same time, and each save is merged into the league. If two GMs grab the same thing in the same moment (say, the same free agent), the first one gets it and the other phone refreshes — keeping that GM's card, lock-in and private plans. Two GMs forming different tag teams or factions at once both stick.
- **End it:** **Office → End party night** (or the link in the banner). The league goes back to taking turns from exactly where it is. GMs who already went live that week stay locked in, and everyone else goes live on their own turn. If someone has to leave early, end party night and they book their show later with turn alerts as usual.
- Needs the Firebase setup in [ONLINE-SETUP.md](ONLINE-SETUP.md) and an internet connection on every device. No new setup beyond online leagues.

## Broadcast touches
- **Title changes** get a full-screen "AND NEW" moment with the real belt (from your belt photos) or a gold nameplate, in the solo results reveal and on the party-night TV.
- **✂️ Photo cut-outs** (Photo Studio, on by default): every new wrestler photo has its background removed on your device (two models together — one finds the subject, one knows what a person is, so suits and gear stay in; on an iPhone, long-press → **Copy Subject** and paste gives the cleanest result of all) and is set on the same card backdrop as the photo-less silhouettes (that wrestler's style color), so the whole roster matches. A PNG that's already a cut-out (see-through background) is used as is. **Cut out existing photos** runs through the photos already on the device and the shared repo photos. The tool (about 15MB) downloads when it runs and isn't stored on the device, so cutting out needs a connection; if it can't run, the photo is kept as it was. ## Your games
Tap **⇄** at the top to see every game on this device — several solo games plus any online leagues — and switch between them. When you have more than one, the game opens on this list (tap outside it to carry on where you left off).

**Move a game to your home-screen app or another device:** in ⇄ Your games, tap **⋯ → Move**. A league gives you its seat code; a solo game makes a one-time move code. Open the game where you want it, tap **⇄ → Receive a game**, and paste the code. Mid-draft is fine.

## Your saves
Turn on **Office → Cloud save** to back up automatically and continue on any device with your sync code (needs the one-time Firebase setup in [ONLINE-SETUP.md](ONLINE-SETUP.md)). The game also keeps automatic backups of your last few weeks under **Office → Backups**.

- **Every solo game syncs both ways.** Each game has its own slot under your sync code. Enter the code on another device and all your games appear in ⇄ Your games there; play any of them anywhere and the others catch up.
- **Saving stays safe.** Everything saves on the device first and the cloud catches up in the background (offline play queues). An upload only lands if nobody else saved that game since this device last synced it, so nothing is overwritten blindly. If both devices played the same game before syncing, you're asked which save to keep — never decided for you — and the other is kept as a backup. A newer cloud save never replaces unsynced play on this device, the game you're playing gets a backup first, and nothing swaps while you're mid-show or mid-edit.
- **Deleting is reversible.** Deleting a game removes it from your other devices, but its cloud copy stays: **Backups → Deleted games** brings it back everywhere. A device that played a game after it was deleted elsewhere keeps it and puts it back.
- **Updating from v195 or earlier** is automatic: the game cloud save used to follow keeps one shared id on every device, games that already had a cloud copy are matched up instead of uploaded twice, and a device still on an older version keeps syncing until it updates. No Firebase rule changes are needed.

Saves and photos live on each device, inside the installed app. The installed app does **not** share saves with a copy opened from Files or a browser tab, so use **Office → Export** in the old copy and **Import** in the new one to move a season over (and **Export Photo Pack / Import Photo Pack** for pictures). Exporting now and then is a good backup.
